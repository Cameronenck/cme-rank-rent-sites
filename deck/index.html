Local Expertise

Why Peoria Decks Need Special Attention

Central Illinois isn’t like the coasts. Our climate, soil, and housing stock all create unique demands on outdoor structures that out-of-town contractors routinely miss.

Freeze-Thaw Cycles

Peoria averages 100+ freeze-thaw cycles per year. Inferior fasteners and boards fail at the joint. We spec hardware rated for Zone 5b and use proper expansion gaps throughout.

Soil & Grading

Peoria-area clay soil heaves and settles seasonally. We dig footings below the frost line (42 inches minimum) and use tube forms or helical piers for lasting stability.

Older Home Ledger Attachment

Most Peoria homes were built between 1940 and 1975. Attaching a deck ledger to these houses requires careful inspection of rim joists and often sistering old lumber before fastening.

Humidity & UV Exposure

Central Illinois summers combine intense UV with high humidity. We recommend finishes and wood species rated for this combination — not whatever is cheapest at the big-box store.

Common Questions About Deck Building in Peoria

Most standard decks (200–400 sq ft) take 3–5 days of active construction once materials are on-site. Factor in 1–2 weeks for permitting and material lead times. Complex builds with multiple levels, built-in seating, or specialty composite materials can take 7–10 construction days. We give you a realistic timeline in your written estimate — not an optimistic one.

Yes — decks in Peoria that are attached to the house or more than 30 inches off the ground require a building permit. We handle the permit application for you. Requirements vary slightly across the surrounding municipalities (Pekin, Morton, East Peoria each have their own processes), but we’re familiar with all of them. Never let a contractor skip the permit — it creates problems when you sell your home.

In the Peoria market, a basic pressure-treated deck typically runs $18–$32 per square foot installed. Composite decks (TimberTech, Trex) run $35–$65 per square foot. A 300 sq ft PT wood deck typically lands between $6,000 and $9,500. A comparable composite deck runs $11,000–$18,000. These are real local ranges — the national averages you see online are usually off for Central Illinois. We give you a fixed-price written quote before any work starts.

For most Peoria homeowners, composite is the better long-term investment if you plan to stay in your home 10+ years. Composite eliminates the cost and hassle of restaining every 2–3 years, resists the freeze-thaw damage that splits PT boards, and holds its appearance better in Central Illinois humidity. If budget is the primary constraint or you’re planning to sell in the next few years, pressure-treated is a perfectly reasonable choice. We’ll give you honest advice based on your situation.
