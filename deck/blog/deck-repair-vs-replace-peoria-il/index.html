Deck Repair vs. Replace in Peoria IL: How to Make the Right Call for Your Home

Published: September 14, 2026 | Read time: 7 minutes

Every Central Illinois homeowner eventually faces the same question: is it worth patching this deck, or is it time to start over? A sagging board here, a soft spot there — the answer depends on what's actually wrong underneath. Here's how to decide, with real Peoria-area pricing so you can weigh the numbers yourself.

When Repair Is the Right Call

Repair makes sense when the damage is in the surface, not the skeleton. If your posts, beams, and joists are still solid and the problems are a handful of loose boards, popped fasteners, or worn stain, a deck repair will get you years more life for a fraction of replacement cost.

The clearest repair candidates are decks under 15 years old where the framing was properly flashed and the structure drains well. A few cracked boards or a faded finish is maintenance, not a death sentence. Full resurfacing — replacing every board while keeping the frame — runs $3,500 to $9,000 for a mid-size deck, far less than a rebuild.

When Replacement Makes More Sense

Replacement wins when the frame itself is failing. Rotted posts, a beam that sags in the middle, a ledger board pulling away from the house, or soft spots in multiple joists are structural problems that patch repairs will not fix for long. You end up paying to repair the same deck every couple of years.

If an inspection finds rot in more than one or two spots, the honest recommendation is usually new deck construction. A replacement built with current materials and proper flashing resets the clock for 20 to 30 years instead of buying you two or three.

The Freeze-Thaw Factor in Central Illinois

Peoria gets 30 to 40 freeze-thaw cycles every winter. Water seeps into cracks and fastener holes, freezes and expands, and slowly splits the wood from the inside. A deck that has absorbed fifteen winters of this has far less strength left than its age suggests.

This is why the repair-or-replace math is different here than in milder climates. A deck that would be a simple repair in Texas or Florida is often a rebuild in Peoria, because the repeated expansion has compromised the structural lumber, not just the surface boards.

What Deck Repair Costs in Peoria

Local repair pricing helps you compare against the cost of starting over:

  • Single board replacement: $150–$400 per board
  • Partial resurfacing (10–20 boards): $800–$2,500
  • Full resurfacing, keep the framing: $3,500–$9,000
  • Structural post or beam repair: $500–$2,000 per issue
  • Ledger replacement with flashing: $800–$2,500
  • Full railing replacement: $2,500–$7,000

What a New Deck Costs in Peoria

A new pressure-treated wood deck runs roughly $6,000 to $9,500 for a 10x12 and $20,000 to $32,000 for a 20x24. Composite runs higher — $10,000 to $16,000 for a 10x12 up to $38,000 to $58,000 for a 20x24 — but with far less long-term upkeep in our climate. Budget an extra $800 to $2,500 to tear out and haul away the old deck.

If you are replacing, this is the moment to consider composite decking, which shrugs off freeze-thaw cycles better than wood and never needs staining.

A 5-Minute Structural Self-Check

Walk your deck and answer a few questions before you call anyone:

  • Press a screwdriver into the boards in several spots. Does it sink anywhere?
  • Does the deck bounce or feel springy when you walk across it?
  • Shake the railing. Is there movement at the posts?
  • Look where the deck meets the house. Any gap, water staining, or separated flashing?
  • Get underneath and check the joists and beam for rot, mold, or sagging.

Soft spots, springiness, or a compromised ledger all point toward replacement rather than a patch job.

Older Peoria Homes and Building Codes

Many Peoria-area homes were built in the 1960s through 1980s, and a lot of decks were added later without the footing depths, flashing, and ledger connections today's code requires. If your deck is an aging add-on, it may not meet current standards even if it looks fine from the top.

That matters for two reasons: safety, and resale. A deck that fails inspection when you sell the house becomes a last-minute expense. If your deck is old enough that the ledger was attached without proper flashing, replacement with current code compliance is usually the safer, smarter long-term move.

The short version: Surface wear is a repair. Structural rot is a replacement. When in doubt, an inspection costs nothing and tells you which path is actually cheaper over the next ten years.
