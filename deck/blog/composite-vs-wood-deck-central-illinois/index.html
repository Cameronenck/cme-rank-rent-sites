Composite vs Wood Decks: Which Is Best for Central Illinois?

July 2026•Peoria Deck Pros•6 min read

Every homeowner planning a deck eventually asks this question. The honest answer is that both wood and composite work in Central Illinois — but they’re right for different situations. Here’s a direct breakdown from contractors who build both every week in the Peoria area.

How Central Illinois Weather Affects the Decision

Peoria’s climate is harder on outdoor wood structures than many parts of the country. The key factor is freeze-thaw cycling. Each winter, temperatures cross 32°F dozens of times. Moisture that works into unprotected wood fibers expands on freezing and contracts on thawing. Over time, this causes cupping, cracking, and fastener loosening even in well-built decks.

The other factor is UV. Central Illinois summers bring significant sun exposure that breaks down wood surface fibers and stain/sealer coatings faster than moderate climates.

Composite decking was engineered specifically to handle these conditions. The wood fiber in composite is fully encapsulated by polymer, so moisture infiltration is minimal. Premium composite products handle our freeze-thaw cycles well and hold their appearance under UV exposure without staining.

The Honest Case for Pressure-Treated Wood

PT wood has one major advantage: upfront cost. A wood deck costs roughly half what an equivalent composite deck costs in the Peoria market. For homeowners who are budget-constrained, or who plan to sell the house before the long-term maintenance cost of wood starts to add up, wood is a legitimate choice.

Built correctly — proper footings, quality fasteners, appropriate wood species, stained or sealed on schedule — a PT deck in Central Illinois can last 20 years. The “built correctly” part matters. A wood deck on undersized or improperly treated framing won’t make it.

  • Lower initial cost ($14k–$22k installed for a standard raised deck vs. $26k–$40k in composite)
  • Easier to repair individual boards
  • Natural wood feel underfoot and visually
  • Works well if you’re comfortable with a maintenance schedule

The Honest Case for Composite

The main advantage of composite is what you don’t have to do. No staining, no sealing, no annual scrubbing of mildew off boards, no replacing individual boards as they cup or crack. You hose it off. Over a 15-year window, the total cost of composite — including the premium upfront cost — often works out close to or below the total cost of a wood deck with proper maintenance included.

The break-even for composite vs. wood in Central Illinois is typically around year 8–12, depending on the wood deck’s maintenance history.
  • Dramatically lower maintenance over time
  • Holds appearance much better through Peoria winters
  • No splinters — important for households with kids or bare feet
  • Better resale signal for buyers who know what they’re looking at
  • 25–35 year lifespan with proper installation

Who Each Option Is Right For

Wood Is Right For You If...

  • Budget is the primary constraint
  • You’re comfortable maintaining it every 2-3 years
  • You prefer natural wood aesthetics
  • The deck is smaller or simpler
  • You plan to sell within 5-7 years

Composite Is Right For You If...

  • You want to set it and forget it
  • Kids and barefoot use are a priority
  • You’re building for the long term
  • The deck is larger or more complex
  • You want to maximize resale value

Our Recommendation for Most Peoria Homeowners

If budget allows, composite. The math works out over time, the maintenance reduction is real, and the performance in our climate is noticeably better. If budget is tight, a properly built PT deck with a committed maintenance schedule is a solid choice and will last well in Central Illinois.
