Deck Cost vs Value: ROI on Your Peoria IL Deck Investment

August 2026•Peoria Deck Pros•7 min read

A deck is a major home investment in Peoria. Most homeowners ask the same question: what will it cost, and will I get the money back when I sell? The answer depends on the type of deck you build, how well you maintain it, and the Central Illinois real estate market at the time of sale. Here's what you need to know.

Average Deck Cost in Peoria IL — 2026 Pricing

Deck pricing in Central Illinois has four main drivers: materials (wood vs composite), size, complexity, and local labor rates. Peoria County sits in the lower-to-middle range nationally — not as expensive as coastal markets, but higher than rural areas of the Midwest.

Pressure-Treated Wood

$18–26 per sq ft

Most affordable option. Requires regular maintenance: staining every 2–3 years, annual sealing. Good for budget-conscious homeowners who don't mind upkeep.

  • 16x12 deck (192 sq ft): $3,456–$4,992
  • 20x16 deck (320 sq ft): $5,760–$8,320

Composite Decking

$28–42 per sq ft

Composite (Trex, TimberTech) costs more upfront but requires minimal maintenance. No staining. lasts 25–30 years with occasional cleaning. Popular choice for Peoria homeowners.

  • 16x12 deck (192 sq ft): $5,376–$8,064
  • 20x16 deck (320 sq ft): $8,960–$13,440

These are materials plus labor for a standard single-level deck with basic railing. Upgrades (custom railings, built-in benches, pergolas, multi-level) add $2,000–$8,000 or more.

What Affects Deck Pricing in Central Illinois

Beyond material choice, several factors move the needle on your final cost:

  • Frost line and footings: Peoria's frost line is 42–48 inches deep. Footings must go that deep to prevent frost heave in winter. Sites with poor drainage or rocky soil can run $500–$1,500 higher due to extra excavation and prep.
  • Existing site prep: Clearing vegetation, removing an old deck, or grading the yard adds 10–20% to the final bid. Level ground is fastest (and cheapest). Sloped yards or sites with multiple trees require more labor.
  • Access and location: A deck accessible from the driveway costs less than one requiring hand-carries from a back gate. Similarly, decks in town (Peoria proper, East Peoria) have slightly lower labor rates than rural Tazewell or Woodford County builds.
  • Railing complexity: Basic aluminum or composite railings are standard. Custom wood railings or cable railings add $8–$15 per linear foot. A deck with 70 linear feet of railing could jump $560–$1,050 just for upgraded railings.
  • Season: Summer and early fall see higher demand — contractors may quote 15–25% premium for faster scheduling. Spring and late fall offer slightly better rates.

Composite vs Wood: Total Cost of Ownership

While composite costs 50–70% more upfront, the maintenance difference over 15 years favors composite by a significant margin.

Material Initial Cost Annual Maintenance 15-Year Total
Pressure-Treated Wood (20x16) $6,500 $300–$450 (stain/seal) $11,000–$12,250
Composite (20x16) $11,200 $100–$150 (cleaning) $12,700–$13,450

The gap narrows significantly over time. After 15 years, a composite deck has a total cost nearly equal to wood when you factor in all the staining, sealing, repairs, and occasional board replacement required for wood. Plus, composite looks newer longer — which matters when selling.

Deck ROI When You Sell Your Peoria Home

Here's the harder question: how much of your deck cost will you recoup when you sell?

National real estate data (Remodeling Magazine 2026) shows that a midrange deck recaptures 50–75% of cost at sale. In Peoria, that sits closer to the middle-to-higher end due to the strong appeal of outdoor living in Central Illinois — long summers, desirable waterfront and suburban locations, and strong buyer appetite for move-in-ready homes.

Realistic ROI Scenarios in Central Illinois

  • Wood deck ($6,500): Expect to recoup $3,250–$4,875 if sold within 5–7 years in good condition. After 10 years, recapture drops to 30–40% due to visible weathering and needed restoration.
  • Composite deck ($11,200): More likely to recoup 60–70% ($6,720–$7,840) even after 10 years. Composite's durability and low maintenance make it more attractive to buyers. A well-maintained composite deck is a genuine selling point.
  • Premium deck ($16,000–$20,000): High-end builds with multiple levels, custom railings, or built-ins recapture 40–60% depending on market timing and condition. They appeal to buyers seeking luxury, but the niche is smaller.

When a Deck Adds Real Value — And When It Doesn't

Your ROI hinges on these factors:

Condition at Sale

A deck in excellent condition (cleaned, sealed, no visible rot, solid railings) adds full perceived value. A deck showing age (weathered, splintered boards, loose railings) signals $2,000–$5,000 in repair costs to the buyer — and they'll negotiate down accordingly. A deteriorated wood deck can actually hurt your sale price more than it helps.

Alignment with Buyer Preferences

In Peoria and the surrounding towns (Pekin, East Peoria, Morton, Dunlap, Washington), outdoor entertaining is a selling feature — especially for families and retirees. A composite deck appeals broadly. A wood deck in poor condition appeals to handypeople only.

Market Timing

Seller's markets (low inventory, high demand) mean decks command closer to full value. Buyer's markets mean buyers have more negotiating power and may not pay a premium for a deck you built. Peoria's market in 2026 has been moderately competitive — neutral ground for deck ROI.

Property Type

A deck is a bigger value-add for suburban single-family homes and acreage than for townhomes or condos. Peoria County has a strong detached-home market, so deck ROI here is solid compared to denser urban markets.

The Real Reason to Build a Deck — Enjoyment, Not Just Investment

If you're strictly focused on ROI, a deck breaks roughly even or slightly positive over 10–15 years. But that's not why most homeowners build one. You're buying 5–15 years of weekend entertaining, family meals outdoors, and a front-row seat to Peoria's spring and fall weather.

A composite deck you'll enjoy for 10 years and then recoup 60% of the cost on sale is genuinely a good investment — both financially and for quality of life. A wood deck you neglect for 8 years and sell in poor condition is neither.
