Why Peoria Decks Need Attention After a Few Winters

Central Illinois weather is hard on outdoor wood structures. Each winter, temperatures cycle above and below freezing dozens of times. Moisture works into the wood grain, expands when it freezes, and contracts when it thaws. Do this for three or four winters and even well-built pressure-treated decks start to show the results: boards that cup or warp, fasteners that back out, posts that move slightly on their footings, and ledger connections that loosen.

The good news: most of this is repairable. A deck that looks worn out is often structurally sound underneath, and a targeted repair — replaced boards, refastened ledger, fresh stain — can extend the life of the structure by a decade for a fraction of the cost of a new build.

Types of Deck Repair We Handle

Decking Board Replacement

Individual boards that have cupped, cracked, or rotted. Matched to existing material when possible, or full deck resurface when widespread.

Structural & Post Repair

Posts showing rot at the base, beams that have checked or cracked, joist damage from moisture and insects. We get under the deck and look.

Ledger Repair & Flashing

The ledger is the board connecting your deck to the house. Improper flashing causes rot behind it. We identify and fix the water intrusion source, not just the symptom.

Railing Replacement

Wood railings that wobble, rot at posts, or fail to meet current code. Replace with wood, composite, or aluminum railing systems.

Stair Repair

Stringers that have cracked or deteriorated, treads that have failed, or stair systems that have shifted off their footings.

Full Deck Resurfacing

When boards are too far gone across the whole surface, we remove all decking and replace it while keeping the structural framing — a cost-effective middle ground between repair and rebuild.

How We Assess a Deck Before We Quote

We don’t quote deck repairs from photos or descriptions. We come to the property and do a physical inspection of the entire structure — not just the surface boards you can see. That means:

  • Checking posts at the soil line (where rot hides first)
  • Inspecting the ledger and the flashing behind it
  • Probing joists and beams for soft spots
  • Looking at fastener condition throughout the framing
  • Checking footing condition where visible
  • Reviewing railing connections for stability

After the inspection, you get a written quote that separates cosmetic repairs from structural ones. You decide what to fix and when — we don’t manufacture urgency.

When to Repair vs. When to Rebuild

Not every tired deck is worth repairing. General guideline: if the structural framing (posts, beams, joists) is sound and the ledger attachment is intact, repair almost always makes financial sense. If framing is compromised in multiple places, or footings have heaved significantly, the cost of repair can approach or exceed a new build — and a new build is the better long-term value.

We give you an honest assessment of which category your deck falls into. We don’t push repairs on decks that should be rebuilt, and we don’t push rebuilds on decks that are perfectly repairable.

  • Structural post/beam repair: $500–$2,000 per issue
  • Ledger replacement with proper flashing: $800–$2,500
  • Full railing replacement (mid-size deck): $2,500–$7,000
