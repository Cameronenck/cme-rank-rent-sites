How to Choose the Right Balloon Decor for Your Event

2026 • Lancaster Float Co • 4 min read

Start With the Space

Before you think about style, think about the venue. The space shapes everything else. A small living room birthday party has different needs than a 200-guest wedding reception at a Lancaster County barn. A private room at a restaurant has different ceiling heights and anchor points than a church fellowship hall. Knowing your venue — its dimensions, its existing aesthetic, and what it will and won't allow — is the single most important factor in choosing the right balloon installation.

If you're not sure what the venue will accommodate, tell us where the event is when you call. We've worked in a lot of Lancaster County spaces and we'll know the constraints or help you figure them out.

Arch vs. Garland: When to Choose Which

This is the most common question we get. Here's the practical breakdown:

Choose an arch when: You want a statement piece with a clear span — two anchor points framing an entrance, a dessert table, or a ceremony backdrop. Arches are most impactful when there's space for the full span and guests will walk toward or through them.

Choose a garland when: You want something that drapes and flows along a surface — a dessert table edge, a staircase rail, a mantle, or a backdrop that needs more organic movement. Garlands are more flexible in where they can go, and they often photograph better from close range.

Many installations use both — an arch as the main focal point and a smaller garland on the dessert table. They don't have to be either/or.

How Big Should It Be?

Bigger isn't always better. An oversized arch in a small space feels overwhelming and closes the room in. A small garland in a large venue disappears. The right scale fits the space.

For most home parties and smaller event spaces, a 6–8 foot arch or 8–12 foot garland is the right range. For larger venues — barn wedding, large church hall, outdoor reception — you want something that reads from a distance, which usually means 10+ feet wide and 8+ feet tall for an arch.

Color: Match the Event, Not Just the Trend

Color choice is personal, but there are some practical guides. If there are existing design elements at the event — linens, florals, invitations, a dress — the balloon palette should complement those, not compete. If you're working with a solid color scheme, you have more freedom to be creative within the palette.

When in doubt, go more muted. Bright, saturated colors photograph beautifully in controlled conditions but can fight with natural light in outdoor or heavily windowed spaces. Muted palettes — dusty rose, sage, ivory, terracotta — hold up in a wider range of lighting situations.

Indoor vs. Outdoor Events

Outdoor balloon installations require different planning than indoor ones. Wind is the main concern — even a light breeze will move an installation that wasn't designed for outdoor conditions. Summer heat in Central Pennsylvania also affects how long balloons hold their shape. For outdoor events in Lancaster County, we typically recommend installing the day of the event and using heavier latex and appropriate anchoring.

For late spring and summer outdoor events — which are common in Lancaster County — tell us when you call that the installation is outdoors. We'll design accordingly.

When to Call

The earlier the better, especially for spring and summer events. But even with shorter lead times, we can often accommodate small to medium installations. Call 717-537-0549 with your event date and venue and we'll tell you what's available and what makes sense.
