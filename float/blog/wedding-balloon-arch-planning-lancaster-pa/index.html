How to Plan a Wedding Balloon Arch in Lancaster County, PA

A practical guide to choosing, styling, and placing a balloon arch that fits your venue, palette, and the look of your day.

September 14, 2026  •  Lancaster Float Co.
Weddings  •  6 min read

Between the ceremony, the cocktail hour, and the reception, a wedding is really a series of backdrops. A well-placed balloon arch gives each one a focal point — something guests photograph, something that frames the vows, something that makes the room feel finished without overwhelming it. If you're planning a wedding in Lancaster County — whether in a restored barn outside Lititz, a garden venue near Ephrata, or a historic hall in downtown Lancaster — a balloon arch can be the single decor element that ties everything together.

The key is planning it with intention rather than treating it as an afterthought. Here's how to do it right.

Start With Your Venue

Your venue should drive the design more than any trend. Lancaster County weddings happen in a wonderfully wide range of spaces, and each one asks for something different. A rustic barn in Manheim with high timber ceilings can carry a large, dramatic arch over the ceremony backdrop or the sweetheart table. A more intimate garden setting near Ephrata might call for something softer and lower, tucked into a corner rather than towering over the space.

Think about where guests will naturally gather and where photos will be taken. The ceremony backdrop and the entrance to the reception are the two highest-impact spots. Ask your venue about ceiling height, available anchor points, and any restrictions on freestanding structures — many historic venues in Lancaster County have guidelines worth knowing before you commit to a size.

Choose a Style That Fits the Day

The two broad directions are organic and structured. Organic arches use mixed balloon sizes in flowing, asymmetrical clusters — they feel soft, romantic, and photograph beautifully. Structured arches are symmetrical and precise, with a more formal, polished look. For most weddings in Lancaster County, couples lean organic, often with greenery, dried florals, or pampas grass woven through the garland for a look that blends balloon work with natural elements.

You can also decide between a full arch (balloons on both sides meeting at the top), a half-arch or asymmetrical sweep (a single flowing side), and a balloon wall or backdrop. A full arch anchors a ceremony; a half-arch frames a dessert table or gift station without stealing the show.

Pick a Palette That Complements Your Colors

Your arch should feel like part of the wedding, not a separate decoration. Pull two or three colors from your existing palette — your florals, bridesmaid dresses, or invitation suite — and build the arch around them. Soft, muted tones are perennially popular for Lancaster weddings: ivory, blush, sage, dusty blue, and warm champagne all read as elegant and photograph well in both barn and garden light.

Metallics work best as accents. A handful of gold or champagne chrome balloons scattered through an ivory and blush garland adds a premium feel without tipping into flashy. If you're unsure, a neutral base with one accent color is the safest, most timeless choice.

Decide on Size and Placement Early

Size is where couples most often go wrong — either too small to register in photos or too large for the space. A useful rule of thumb: the arch should frame, not block. If it sits behind the couple during the ceremony, make sure it's wide enough that guests can still see both of you clearly. If it's a photo backdrop for the reception, give it enough room that people can stand in front of it without crowding the dessert table.

Placement matters for flow, too. An arch at the reception entrance welcomes guests and sets the tone immediately. One behind the sweetheart table gives you a built-in backdrop for speeches and toasts. Many Lancaster County couples use a single arch and have it moved between ceremony and reception — just plan that transition with your venue coordinator ahead of time.

Plan for Central PA Weather

Lancaster County weather can shift quickly, especially in spring and fall. If your arch will live outdoors — say, for a ceremony in a Lititz garden or a tented reception near Manheim — it needs to be anchored properly and built to handle a breeze. Quality balloons treated for outdoor use and sturdy framing matter more than you'd expect on a windy afternoon. For fully outdoor weddings, most couples schedule the arch close to the event start so it looks its freshest when guests arrive.

Indoor arches avoid the weather question entirely and stay pristine all day. If any part of your timeline is outdoors, have a conversation about placement, anchoring, and timing during the planning phase rather than the week of.

Book Early, Especially in Peak Season

Wedding season in Lancaster County — roughly May through October — fills up quickly, and the most popular Saturday dates are often reserved well in advance. If your date is set, reach out early so you're not scrambling. Booking ahead also gives time to refine the design, confirm colors with your florist, and coordinate installation timing with your venue.

Planning a wedding balloon arch doesn't have to be complicated, but it does reward a little forethought. Start with the venue, choose a style and palette that feel like you, and let the arch do what it does best: give every moment of your day a beautiful frame.
