Furnace Noises in Cold Weather

What is normal, what is not, and which sounds mean you should call a licensed technician before heating season fills the schedule.

Home / Blog / Furnace Noises in Cold Weather
Published: September 28, 2026

Every furnace makes some sound. The first cold snap of the year in Ohio is also the first time many homeowners have listened closely to their heating system since last winter, and a furnace that was silent all summer suddenly has a voice. Some of what you hear is normal. Some of it is a part telling you it is about to fail. Knowing the difference is what keeps a small noise from becoming a cold night and an emergency service call. Homeowners in Columbus, Cleveland, Cincinnati, Toledo, and Akron hear the same set of noises every fall.

Start With the Sounds That Are Actually Normal

A healthy furnace is audible but not loud. You should be able to stand beside it and hear the following without alarm.

  • A soft whoosh at startup. That is the burners lighting. It should be brief and gentle.
  • A steady low hum. The inducer motor and the blower motor both hum while running. A consistent, quiet hum is exactly what you want.
  • Light ticking as it warms. Metal expands as it heats. A few soft ticks early in the cycle are thermal expansion and nothing more.
  • A quiet click or two at the start. The ignition sequence and gas valve opening produce one or two clicks. That is normal operation.

The line to remember is simple. If the noise is steady, soft, and predictable, it is probably fine. If it is loud enough that you notice it from another room, it is not normal.

The Loud Bang at Startup

A bang or boom when the furnace fires is the most urgent sound on this list. It usually means delayed ignition: gas has accumulated in the burner compartment for a moment before it lights, then ignites all at once instead of in a controlled sequence.

Each bang stresses the heat exchanger. Repeated bangs can crack it, and a cracked heat exchanger is a carbon monoxide risk that turns a repair into a replacement. Causes include dirty burners, a failing ignitor, or incorrect gas pressure. If you hear a bang at startup, shut the furnace off at its power switch and call a licensed technician. This is the one noise where the safe move is to stop using the system until it is diagnosed.

Squealing and Screeching

A high-pitched squeal or screech almost always points to a bearing. The blower motor and the inducer motor both ride on bearings that wear and dry out over years of use. On older furnaces, a worn drive belt can also squeal before it breaks.

This noise gets worse as the motor warms up. A dry bearing gets louder, then seizes, and a seized blower motor leaves you with no heat at all. Servicing a bearing early costs far less than replacing a motor later.

Rumbling and Grumbling

A deep rumble is different from a bang. Where a bang is a single sharp report at ignition, a rumble is a low, ongoing grumble while the burners run. It usually means combustion is not happening cleanly, most often because the burners are dirty or the gas pressure is off.

Rumbling is not normal and should be diagnosed by a professional. Unclean combustion wastes gas and can produce more carbon monoxide than a properly tuned furnace. If a rumble comes with a gas smell or a carbon monoxide alarm, treat it as an emergency, leave the home, and call for service immediately. Rattling and Buzzing

Rattling is usually mechanical, and often the least serious item on this list. A loose cabinet panel, a screw that has worked free, a blower wheel out of balance, or ductwork vibrating against a floor joist will all produce a rattle. Sometimes the fix is as simple as reseating the door panel you opened to change the filter.

One rattle is worth taking seriously though. If the blower wheel has come loose, the imbalance will wear the motor bearing over time and the rattle will grow into a squeal. If reseating the panels and tightening the obvious screws does not quiet it, have the blower assembly checked.

Rapid Clicking That Never Lights

One or two clicks at startup are normal. Rapid, repeated clicking that never results in a flame is a failed ignition sequence. The control board commands the ignitor and gas valve over and over, but the flame sensor does not confirm a flame, so the board shuts the gas valve and tries again.

Modern furnaces lock out for safety after three failed attempts. If you hear the click-click-click pattern three times and then silence, your furnace has locked out. This is typically a failed ignitor or a dirty flame sensor, both of which a technician can diagnose using the control board fault code. What You Can Safely Check Yourself

Before calling anyone, there are a few safe steps a homeowner can take. None involve opening the burner compartment or the blower assembly.

  • Reseat the panels. A panel not fully seated on its safety switch is a common source of rattles and no-heat conditions after a filter change.
  • Check the filter. A filter that has collapsed or is vibrating loose can rattle and can restrict airflow enough to make the blower work harder and louder.
  • Tighten obvious screws. Cabinet screws loosen over time. A gentle pass with a screwdriver quiets a surprising number of rattles.
  • Note the pattern. Write down when the noise happens and whether it is constant or cyclical. This shortens the visit and often lets the technician bring the right part on the first trip.

Do not open the burner compartment, touch the gas valve, or bypass a safety switch to quiet a noise. Those actions risk carbon monoxide exposure and void your warranty.

Frequently Asked Questions

What furnace noises are normal on a cold Ohio night?

A soft whoosh when the burners light, a low hum from the inducer and blower, a light tick as metal expands, and one or two clicks at startup are all normal. If the noise is loud enough that you notice it from another room, it is worth investigating.

Why is my furnace making a loud banging noise?

A loud bang at startup is usually delayed ignition, where gas builds up before it lights and then ignites all at once. It is the most urgent furnace noise because repeated bangs stress and can crack the heat exchanger. Shut the furnace off and call a licensed technician.

What causes a squealing or screeching furnace sound?

Squealing almost always means a bearing is failing in the blower motor, the inducer motor, or a worn belt on older furnaces. It gets louder as the motor warms up. Servicing the bearing early is far cheaper than replacing a seized motor later.

Is a rumbling furnace sound dangerous?

A deep rumble usually means the burners are running rich or the burner assembly is dirty, so combustion is not happening cleanly. It wastes gas and can produce more carbon monoxide. Rumbling is not normal and should be diagnosed by a professional.

Why does my furnace rattle when it runs?

Rattling is usually mechanical and often the least serious noise. Common causes are a loose panel, a free screw, ductwork vibrating against a joist, or an unbalanced blower wheel. A loose blower wheel should be fixed promptly because imbalance wears the motor bearing.

What does clicking mean when my furnace starts?

One or two clicks at startup are normal ignition and gas valve operation. Rapid clicking that never ends in a flame is a failed ignition sequence, usually a bad ignitor or a flame sensor that cannot prove flame. Three failed attempts and the furnace locks out.

Can I fix furnace noises myself?

You can safely reseat panels, tighten rattling screws, and check the filter. Anything inside the combustion path, the blower assembly, or the gas valve should be left to a licensed technician. Never bypass a safety switch to quiet a noise.

When is a furnace noise an emergency?

Treat it as an emergency if you smell gas, the carbon monoxide alarm sounds, you hear a loud bang at startup, or you see soot or scorching. Shut the furnace off, leave the home, and call for emergency service or your gas utility. A squeal or rattle on a mild day can wait for an appointment.

Will a fall tune-up prevent furnace noise problems?

It catches most of them early. A tune-up lubricates and inspects the motors, checks the burners, verifies gas pressure, cleans the flame sensor, tests the ignitor, and confirms the heat exchanger is sound. Most loud furnace noises in Ohio trace back to something a September tune-up would have flagged.

How much does it cost to fix a noisy furnace in Ohio?

It depends on the cause. A panel reseat or filter fix is minimal. Blower motor replacement typically runs 400 to 800 dollars installed, while a bearing service or belt replacement on an older furnace is often 150 to 350 dollars. Getting a diagnosis before heating season peaks is far cheaper than an emergency call in January.
