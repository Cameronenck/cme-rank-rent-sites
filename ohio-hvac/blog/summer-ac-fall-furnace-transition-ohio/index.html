Summer AC to Fall Furnace Transition: Ohio HVAC Checklist 2026

Published August 27, 2026 | Ohio HVAC Pros

Late summer marks the perfect time to prepare for Ohio's transition from cool air conditioning to heating season. Whether you're switching from AC to furnace or adjusting your thermostat for cooler fall nights, proper planning ensures seamless operation and prevents costly breakdowns when you need heat most.

Why the AC-to-Furnace Transition Matters in Ohio

Ohio experiences dramatic seasonal shifts. Summer air conditioning runs hard from June through August, then your furnace takes over starting in late September through early spring. This transition period is critical—many homeowners neglect maintenance, assuming their systems will work fine. But HVAC systems sitting idle all summer need attention before they're called into service again.

A furnace that hasn't been checked since spring could have dust buildup, loose connections, or component issues that go undetected until cold weather arrives. By then, emergency repairs cost 2-3 times more than routine maintenance, and you'll be uncomfortable in your home while waiting for service.

The AC Shutdown Checklist

Step 1: Set Your AC to Off Mode

As temperatures cool in late September, stop using your air conditioner regularly. Set your thermostat to heat mode instead of cooling. While you might occasionally need AC on warm fall days, continuous AC operation after September wastes energy and strains the compressor.

Step 2: Switch Off the Outdoor Condenser Unit

Once you've switched fully to heating mode, turn off the AC condenser unit outdoors at the breaker switch or disconnect box. Leaving it on wastes electricity and unnecessarily stresses the system during fall's mild temperatures.

Step 3: Cover Your Outdoor Unit

Protect your AC condenser from winter weather by installing a weatherproof cover. This shields it from rain, snow, ice, and debris that can damage coils and internal components. A simple cover (under $20) extends the life of your AC unit significantly.

Step 4: Have a Professional Check Before Shutdown

If you're shutting down your AC for an extended period, ask your HVAC technician to verify that refrigerant is properly sealed and the system is clean. This prevents corrosion and ensures smooth startup next spring.

The Furnace Startup Checklist

Replace Your Furnace Air Filter First

If you haven't replaced your furnace filter since spring, now is the time. A filter that's been sitting all summer has likely collected dust, pet hair, and other debris. A fresh filter ensures clean airflow and prevents your furnace from working harder than necessary when heating season starts.

Schedule a Professional Furnace Inspection

Before cold weather arrives, hire a licensed HVAC technician for a comprehensive furnace inspection. This is far more thorough than your own visual check. A professional inspection includes:

  • Cleaning and inspecting burners for proper combustion
  • Checking the heat exchanger for cracks or corrosion
  • Testing all safety controls and ignition systems
  • Inspecting the blower motor and cleaning components
  • Measuring airflow and system efficiency
  • Testing thermostat accuracy
  • Checking for carbon monoxide leaks
  • Verifying gas pressure and supply lines

This inspection costs $150-300 but prevents emergencies that cost $5,000+ for mid-winter repairs or furnace replacement.

Test Your Furnace Manually

Before relying on your furnace, test it yourself. Set your thermostat to heat mode and lower the setpoint about 5 degrees below current room temperature. Your furnace should fire up within a minute or two. If it doesn't start, don't wait—call a technician immediately. A furnace that won't start could have ignition issues, gas supply problems, or control board failures.

Thermostat Adjustment and Settings

Switch to Heat Mode

By late September, switch your thermostat setting from Cool to Heat. This ensures your system heats rather than cools when room temperature drops. Modern programmable and smart thermostats make this easy with a simple mode switch.

Verify Thermostat Accuracy

Before relying on your thermostat all winter, verify it reads correctly. Compare your thermostat's temperature display against a standalone thermometer in the same room. If they differ by more than 2 degrees, your thermostat may need calibration or replacement.

Set Your Heating Schedule

If you have a programmable or smart thermostat, set your heating schedule now. Lower the temperature by 7-10 degrees when you're asleep and 10-15 degrees when away from home. This simple change saves 10-15% on heating costs all winter long. Smart thermostats learn your patterns and adjust automatically, making this even easier.

Consider a Smart Thermostat Upgrade

If you have an older manual thermostat, upgrading to a smart thermostat before heating season pays dividends. Smart thermostats track energy use, provide remote control via smartphone apps, and optimize your heating schedule based on weather forecasts and your lifestyle. Many models pay for themselves through energy savings within 1-2 years.

Other Important Transition Tasks

Seal Air Leaks Around Windows and Doors

Before heating season, check all exterior doors and windows for air leaks. Even small gaps waste heating energy significantly. Add weatherstripping to doors and windows, and caulk any cracks around frames. These simple fixes reduce heat loss by 10-20%.

Check Your Home's Insulation

Ohio homes should have at least 12 inches of attic insulation. If yours is older or settling, adding more insulation improves heating efficiency. Also check basement and crawlspace insulation—many older homes lack sufficient insulation in these areas.

Ensure All Vents and Returns Are Clear

Before heating season, verify all furnace return air grilles and supply vents throughout your home are unobstructed. Blocked vents reduce airflow and force your furnace to work harder. Move furniture, curtains, and storage items away from vents.

Have Your Ducts Inspected

If your home has unfinished basement or attic spaces, visually inspect accessible ductwork for visible leaks, gaps, or separations. Leaky ducts waste 20-30% of heating energy. A professional ductwork inspection (and sealing if needed) costs $200-500 but saves far more through improved efficiency.

Common Transition Problems and Solutions

My Furnace Smells Strange When I First Turn It On

A mild burning smell on first startup is normal—dust accumulated in the furnace during summer burns off. This should clear within 5-15 minutes. However, persistent chemical smells, rotten odors, or intense burning warrant immediate professional inspection for safety.

My Thermostat Won't Switch to Heat Mode

If your thermostat won't switch from cooling to heating mode, check the batteries (if applicable), ensure the breaker isn't tripped, and verify the furnace is powered on. If problems persist, call a technician—it could be a faulty thermostat or control issue.

My Furnace Runs Constantly

If your furnace won't stop running when you first turn it on, check that your air filter isn't clogged (this causes overheating and constant cycling) and ensure all return vents are unblocked. Also verify your thermostat setpoint isn't abnormally high. If the problem continues, call a professional to check for more serious issues.

Timing Your Transition

In Ohio, the ideal time to make this transition is late August or early September. Schedule your furnace inspection and tune-up by September 15th at the latest—this ensures everything is ready before the first cold snap. Waiting until October puts you in the rush of heating season when technicians are booked solid and emergency calls cost premium prices.
