September AC to Heating Transition: Prepare Your HVAC System for Fall

Published: September 7, 2026 By Ohio HVAC Pros

As summer winds down and September arrives in Ohio, your HVAC system is about to undergo a significant shift. For months, your air conditioning has been working overtime to keep your home cool through hot, humid days. But now, with cooler fall nights approaching, it's time to transition from cooling to heating mode. Making this shift smoothly is essential to ensure your furnace is ready to handle Ohio's cold winters while keeping your energy bills in check.

Many homeowners overlook the seasonal transition period, but September is the perfect time to prepare your heating system. By taking action now, you can avoid emergency repairs when temperatures drop unexpectedly and ensure your home stays comfortable all winter long.

Why September Matters: Don't Wait Until Winter

September is the ideal window for HVAC preparation in Ohio. Here's why: most people don't think about heating until October or November, when HVAC contractors are slammed with service calls and emergency repairs. By scheduling your fall HVAC maintenance in early September, you'll have your choice of appointment times, and you'll avoid the rush and premium pricing that comes with winter emergencies.

Additionally, a furnace that hasn't run all summer may have accumulated dust, debris, or loose components. Running it now—before you actually need the heat—allows you to catch problems while there's still time to address them without the stress of a cold home.

Key Steps to Transition Your HVAC System

1. Schedule a Professional Furnace Inspection and Tune-Up

The first and most important step is to have a licensed HVAC technician inspect your furnace. During a fall tune-up, your technician will:

  • Check the ignition system and ensure the pilot light operates correctly
  • Inspect heat exchangers for cracks or corrosion
  • Clean or replace furnace filters (a clogged filter can reduce efficiency by up to 15%)
  • Test gas pressure and burner operation
  • Lubricate moving parts to reduce friction and noise
  • Verify safety controls and shutoffs are functioning
  • Inspect ductwork for leaks or blockages

This proactive maintenance can extend your furnace's lifespan, improve efficiency, and prevent costly breakdowns. Most furnaces run more reliably and efficiently after a professional tune-up.

2. Test Your Thermostat

Before relying on your thermostat for heating, test it to ensure it's working properly. Set it to heating mode and confirm that your furnace kicks on at the desired temperature. If you have a programmable or smart thermostat, update your settings for fall—you may want to lower temperatures during the day when no one is home and raise them in the evening.

A faulty thermostat can waste energy and leave you uncomfortable, so addressing this now prevents issues down the road.

3. Clean or Replace Your Air Filter

Your furnace's air filter collects dust, pet hair, and other debris throughout the cooling season. A clogged filter restricts airflow, forcing your furnace to work harder and use more energy. Replace your filter before fall—ideally every 3 months during heating season—to maintain efficiency and indoor air quality.

If anyone in your home has allergies or respiratory issues, consider upgrading to a higher-MERV-rated filter for better air quality during the months when you're running your furnace regularly.

4. Inspect and Clean Your Vents and Returns

Check all return air vents and supply vents in your home. Ensure they're not blocked by furniture, rugs, or curtains. Clean any visible dust or debris from vent covers. Blocked vents reduce heating efficiency and can create cold spots in your home.

5. Check Your Ductwork for Leaks

Leaky ducts can waste up to 20% of your heated air, costing you money and comfort. If you notice areas of your home that are significantly colder than others, you may have ductwork issues. A professional can seal leaks using duct sealant or mastic to improve efficiency and comfort.

Signs Your Heating System Needs Attention

If you notice any of these warning signs, contact a professional HVAC technician right away:

  • Strange noises coming from your furnace (rattling, banging, or squealing)
  • Reduced heating output or uneven temperatures throughout your home
  • Yellow or flickering pilot light (should be blue)
  • Furnace is more than 15 years old and has never been professionally serviced
  • Visible rust or corrosion on your furnace or ductwork
  • Your furnace is cycling on and off frequently
  • Unusual smells or signs of gas leaks

Energy Efficiency Tips for the Heating Season

Once your heating system is ready, you can maximize efficiency and reduce your energy bills:

  • Set your thermostat to 68-70 degrees when home and lower it when away or sleeping
  • Seal air leaks around windows, doors, and attic access points
  • Use ceiling fans to distribute warm air more evenly (run them clockwise on low speed)
  • Add weatherstripping or caulk to reduce drafts
  • Keep vents and returns clear of obstructions
  • Consider a service agreement for annual maintenance to keep your system running smoothly
