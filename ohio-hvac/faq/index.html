HVAC Questions & Answers

Common questions Ohio homeowners ask about HVAC installation, repair, maintenance, and costs. Can't find your answer? Call 614-344-4851 for personalized help.

Full HVAC system replacement in Ohio typically runs $5,000 to $12,000 depending on system type, home size, and equipment brand. A furnace-only replacement averages $3,000 to $6,000, while a central AC unit runs $3,500 to $7,500 installed. Heat pump systems fall in the $4,500 to $10,000 range. Prices vary by region — Columbus and Cleveland metro areas tend to be slightly higher than rural Ohio counties.
Ohio requires HVAC contractors to hold proper licensing through their local jurisdiction. Verify credentials by asking for their license number, checking for liability insurance and workers' compensation coverage, and confirming they pull required permits. Ohio homeowners should schedule HVAC maintenance twice per year — once in spring for the AC system and once in fall for the furnace. Ohio's temperature swings from sub-zero winters to 90-degree summers put heavy demand on HVAC equipment. Regular maintenance extends system life by 5 to 10 years, prevents breakdowns during peak seasons, and keeps your system running at peak efficiency.
HVAC sizing depends on your home's square footage, insulation quality, window count, and local climate zone. As a rough guide, Ohio homes need about 1 ton of cooling capacity per 500 to 600 square feet. A 2,000 square foot Ohio home typically needs a 3 to 3.5 ton system. Undersized systems run constantly and never reach temperature; oversized systems short-cycle and create humidity problems. A qualified contractor should perform a Manual J load calculation before recommending equipment.
Modern cold-climate heat pumps work efficiently down to -10 to -15 degrees Fahrenheit, making them viable throughout Ohio. They provide both heating and cooling from a single unit. Many Ohio homeowners install a heat pump with a gas furnace backup (dual-fuel system) for the coldest stretches. Heat pumps can reduce heating costs 30 to 50 percent compared to straight electric heating and may qualify for federal tax credits up to $2,000.
A well-maintained gas furnace in Ohio typically lasts 15 to 20 years. Electric furnaces can last 20 to 30 years. Factors that shorten furnace life include skipping annual maintenance, running the system with dirty filters, and oversized or undersized equipment. If your furnace is over 15 years old and needing frequent repairs, replacement usually makes more financial sense than continued repairs.
Carrier, Trane, Lennox, Rheem, and Goodman are all widely installed across Ohio. The best brand for your home depends on budget, efficiency targets, and contractor availability. More important than the brand name is proper sizing, correct installation, and regular maintenance. A mid-range system installed correctly will outperform a premium system installed poorly. Ask your contractor which brands they are factory-authorized to install and service.

Emergency HVAC repair in Ohio typically costs $150 to $500 for the service call and repair combined, depending on the issue and time of day. After-hours, weekend, and holiday calls usually carry an additional $75 to $150 surcharge. Common emergency repairs include ignitor or flame sensor replacement ($150 to $300), capacitor replacement ($150 to $350), and blower motor repair ($250 to $600).
As of 2025, the federal minimum SEER2 rating for new AC units in Ohio (Northern region) is 14.3 SEER2. For most Ohio homeowners, a 16 to 18 SEER2 unit offers the best balance of upfront cost and energy savings. Higher-efficiency units (20+ SEER2) can make sense if you plan to stay in the home long-term and have high summer cooling costs. Each SEER point higher typically saves 5 to 8 percent on cooling costs.
A common rule of thumb: if the repair costs more than 50 percent of a new unit and the system is over 10 years old, replacement is usually the better investment. Also consider replacement if the system uses R-22 refrigerant (phased out and expensive), needs frequent repairs, or no longer cools your home evenly. A new high-efficiency system can reduce cooling costs 20 to 40 percent compared to a unit from the early 2000s.
A straightforward furnace or AC replacement in an Ohio home typically takes 4 to 8 hours in a single day. A complete system replacement (furnace plus AC or heat pump) usually takes 1 to 2 days. If ductwork modifications, electrical upgrades, or gas line changes are needed, the job may take 2 to 3 days. Your contractor should provide a timeline estimate before work begins.
Yes. The federal Inflation Reduction Act offers tax credits up to $2,000 for qualifying heat pumps and up to $600 for qualifying furnaces and AC units. Ohio utility companies including AEP Ohio, Duke Energy Ohio, and FirstEnergy also offer rebates for high-efficiency HVAC installations. Rebate amounts change annually, so check with your utility and contractor for current offers before purchasing.
A furnace burns fuel (natural gas, propane, or oil) or uses electric resistance to create heat. A heat pump moves heat from outdoor air into your home using refrigerant — similar to an air conditioner running in reverse. Heat pumps are more energy-efficient than furnaces in mild to moderate cold but lose efficiency as temperatures drop below freezing. Many Ohio homeowners use a dual-fuel setup: a heat pump for temperatures above 30 to 35 degrees and a gas furnace for colder stretches.
