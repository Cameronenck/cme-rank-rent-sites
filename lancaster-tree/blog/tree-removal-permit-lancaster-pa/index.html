Do I Need a Permit to Remove a Tree in Lancaster PA?

Posted: October 5, 2026 | Reading time: 8 min | Service Area: Lancaster PA, Elizabethtown & Lancaster County

One of the first questions Lancaster County homeowners ask before booking a removal is whether they need permission. The answer depends on where the tree stands — your own yard, the strip between the sidewalk and the curb, or inside a historic district. Removing a tree on private property in most of Lancaster County does not require a permit, but street trees, right-of-way trees, and trees in regulated districts often do. Cutting the wrong one without approval can turn a routine project into a fine and a replacement obligation.

Quick Answer: In most of Lancaster County, a tree on your own private property can be removed without a permit. You likely DO need approval if the tree sits in a public right-of-way or tree lawn, is a designated street tree, or stands inside a historic district. When in doubt, call your borough or township office before removal — the call is free and takes five minutes.

The Short Answer for Lancaster County Homeowners

Pennsylvania does not have a statewide tree removal permit for private property. That authority is delegated to municipalities, and rules vary from one borough and township to the next. Lancaster City, Elizabethtown Borough, Lititz, Manheim, and the surrounding townships each write their own ordinances. The consistent principle: what happens on your own lot is largely your call, and what happens in the public right-of-way is regulated.

When You Do NOT Need a Permit in Lancaster PA

For the typical homeowner, removal is straightforward. A tree fully inside your property line — a shade tree in the backyard, a dead maple beside the garage — is almost always yours to remove without a permit. A hazard tree that is actively dangerous, leaning toward a structure or split over a driveway, can usually be removed first with paperwork filed later. And an ordinary landscaping tree you planted away from the right-of-way is not regulated in most Lancaster County townships. Even so, a legitimate removal company confirms property lines and checks for utility conflicts before cutting.

When You DO Need Approval

Three situations trigger a permit or approval requirement across most of the region:

1. Street Trees and Right-of-Way Trees

The strip between the sidewalk and the curb is typically public right-of-way, even though you may mow it. Trees there are often classified as street trees under a municipal shade tree ordinance. Removing one usually requires permission, and some communities require a replacement tree of a specified size. In older Lancaster County boroughs, including Elizabethtown and Lititz, these tree lawns are common.

2. Historic Districts

Lancaster City has designated historic districts, and some surrounding boroughs maintain their own. Within these boundaries, removing a mature tree can require a certificate of appropriateness from a review board.

3. Trees Covered by a Conservation or HOA Rule

Some Lancaster County developments add covenants requiring approval before removing mature trees, and land under a conservation easement may restrict clearing entirely. These are private rules rather than municipal permits, but they carry real consequences.

How to Check Your Specific Property

Five minutes of homework prevents almost every permit problem. Call your municipality — Lancaster City, Elizabethtown Borough, and each township has a code office that can tell you in one call whether your tree is regulated. Measure the distance to the curb; if the trunk sits between the sidewalk and the street, treat it as right-of-way. Check your deed and HOA documents, where covenants and easements live. And ask your arborist, because a reputable crew knows the ordinances in the towns they work.

What Happens If You Skip the Permit

Removing a regulated tree without approval can lead to a stop-work order, a fine that in some Pennsylvania municipalities reaches into the thousands per tree, and a requirement to plant replacements at your own cost. A permit is inexpensive or free, while the penalty is not.

Emergency Removals Are a Different Case

When a tree has fallen on a house or is hanging over a road after a storm, most municipalities allow immediate removal to make the site safe, with notification afterward. No code official expects you to wait on paperwork while a trunk sits on your roof. Document the hazard with photos and follow up once the danger is cleared. Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a permit to remove a tree on my own property in Lancaster PA?

In most cases, no. Pennsylvania does not issue statewide permits for private tree removal, and most Lancaster County municipalities allow you to remove trees on your own lot without approval. The exceptions are street trees, right-of-way trees, and trees inside a historic district.

Who owns the trees between the sidewalk and the street?

That strip is typically public right-of-way, and the trees in it are usually classified as street trees under a municipal shade tree ordinance. Removing one generally requires borough or township approval, and a replacement may be required.

Is a permit required to remove a tree in a historic district of Lancaster?

Lancaster City maintains designated historic districts, and removing a mature tree within one can require a certificate of appropriateness from a review board. Confirm whether your property falls inside a district first.

Can I remove a storm-damaged tree without a permit?

Yes, in an emergency. When a tree is down or actively dangerous, most municipalities allow immediate removal to make the site safe, with notification afterward. Document the hazard and follow up with the local office.

What happens if I remove a regulated tree without approval?

Penalties vary by municipality but can include a stop-work order, a fine that in some Pennsylvania communities reaches into the thousands per tree, and a requirement to plant replacements at your expense.
