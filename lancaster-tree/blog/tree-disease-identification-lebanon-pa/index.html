Tree Disease Identification: How to Spot Sick Trees in Lebanon PA

August 3, 2026 • Lancaster Tree Removal Pros • 30 min read
Published: August 3, 2026 | Topic: Tree Health & Disease | Location: Lebanon PA & Lancaster County

Trees are a valuable asset to any property in Lebanon PA and throughout Lancaster County. They provide shade, privacy, air quality improvement, and aesthetic appeal. But trees are also vulnerable to diseases that can weaken them, make them dangerous, and eventually require professional removal. Learning to identify common tree diseases early can help you save a tree before it becomes a safety hazard — or decide when professional removal is the only safe option.

Why Tree Disease Matters in Lebanon PA

Lebanon PA experiences a humid continental climate with warm summers and cold winters. These conditions are ideal for several tree diseases to thrive, especially fungal infections. Many of the trees common to Lancaster County — oak, maple, ash, elm, and birch — are susceptible to different diseases depending on season and site conditions. Early recognition of symptoms can mean the difference between saving a tree and losing it.

Common Tree Diseases in Lebanon PA and Lancaster County

1. Dutch Elm Disease (DED)

What It Is

Dutch Elm Disease is a fungal infection spread by elm bark beetles. It clogs the tree's water-conducting vessels, causing the tree to essentially starve itself.

Signs to Look For

  • Wilting and yellowing leaves that appear suddenly in spring or early summer
  • Leaf loss starting at the branch tips and moving inward
  • Discolored streaks under the bark (visible when you cut into a branch)
  • Affected branches may die in a single season
  • Browning of the vascular tissue when you make a fresh cut

What To Do

DED spreads rapidly. If you suspect it, call a certified arborist immediately. The tree may need to be removed to prevent spread to nearby elms. Pruning infected branches may help, but if DED has reached the trunk, removal is usually necessary.

2. Oak Wilt

What It Is

Oak Wilt is caused by a fungus that affects red and white oak species differently. Red oaks decline rapidly; white oaks may survive longer but are still at risk.

Signs to Look For

  • Discolored foliage starting at leaf margins and spreading inward
  • Leaves may turn brown, bronze, or red depending on oak species
  • Rapid defoliation — sometimes losing most leaves by mid-summer
  • Wilting and browning can affect the entire crown
  • Brown discoloration under the bark in the sapwood

What To Do

Oak Wilt can kill a red oak in as little as one season. Do not prune infected oaks during growing season (spreads spores). Professional removal may be necessary, especially for red oaks with advanced symptoms.

3. Anthracnose (Leaf Scorch)

What It Is

Anthracnose is a fungal disease that affects many tree species — sycamores, oaks, dogwoods, maples — especially in cool, wet springs.

Signs to Look For

  • Leaf spots or blotches, usually brown with darker edges
  • Veins may appear darker than the rest of the leaf
  • Twig dieback and shoot blight
  • Cankers (sunken areas) on branches and twigs
  • Defoliation if infection is severe

What To Do

Most trees recover from anthracnose on their own after one season. Prune dead twigs and branches to improve air circulation. If the tree repeatedly suffers severe defoliation or the canopy is dying back, consider removal.

4. Emerald Ash Borer (EAB)

What It Is

EAB is not a disease but an invasive beetle that has devastated ash trees across North America, including Lancaster County. The larvae feed under the bark, disrupting nutrient and water flow.

Signs to Look For

  • D-shaped exit holes in the bark (about the size of a match head)
  • Woodpecker damage as birds hunt the larvae
  • Thinning canopy and branch dieback, often starting at the top
  • Vertical bark splits exposing the wood beneath
  • Decline can progress rapidly once visible

What To Do

Once EAB is confirmed, ash trees decline quickly. If the tree is structurally sound and important to your property, a certified arborist might recommend professional treatment. Many homeowners in Lebanon PA choose removal to prevent spread and avoid the inevitable decline.

5. Canker Diseases

What It Is

Cankers are sunken, dead areas on branches or trunks caused by fungi or bacteria. They can eventually girdle a branch or trunk, cutting off water and nutrient flow.

Signs to Look For

  • Sunken, discolored areas on bark
  • Dead bark that may peel or crack
  • Oozing resin or sap (especially on pine, spruce, and fir)
  • Twig or branch dieback beyond the canker
  • Multiple cankers on the same tree indicate a serious problem

What To Do

Remove branches with cankers back to the branch collar. If cankers are on the main trunk or if multiple large cankers are present, the tree may not recover. A certified arborist can assess whether the tree can be saved.

How to Inspect Your Trees for Disease

Early detection is key. Here's how to regularly check your trees for signs of disease:

  • Look from a distance first: Step back and examine the overall shape and density of the canopy. Does it look balanced? Are there obvious gaps or thin areas?
  • Check the leaves: Walk around the tree and observe leaves up close. Look for spots, discoloration, wilting, or unusual appearance.
  • Examine branches: Look for dead branches, cankers, oozing sap, bark damage, or insect galleries.
  • Inspect the trunk: Cracks, sunken areas, peeling bark, or oozing sap are warning signs.
  • Look at the base: Mushrooms, root rot, or damage near the soil line can indicate serious problems.
  • Check for insects: Bark beetles, borers, and scale insects can spread disease or weaken trees.
Pro Tip: Keep a simple log of what you observe each season. Photograph any suspicious areas and note the date. This documentation will help a certified arborist diagnose problems accurately.

When To Call a Professional Arborist in Lebanon PA

You don't need to be an expert to know when to call for help. Here are clear signs you should reach out to a certified arborist or tree removal company:

  • The tree has multiple dead branches or significant canopy thinning
  • You notice rapid decline in a single season
  • Large cankers are present on the trunk or major branches
  • You suspect a specific disease like Dutch Elm Disease or Oak Wilt
  • The tree is declining and you're not sure why
  • A diseased tree threatens your home, power lines, or a neighbor's property
  • The tree is dying and you need professional removal

A certified arborist can perform a detailed health assessment, identify the disease or problem, and recommend the best course of action — whether that's treatment, pruning, or professional removal.

Prevention: Keeping Your Trees Healthy

The best strategy is prevention. Healthy trees are more resistant to disease. Here's how to keep your trees in Lebanon PA strong:

  • Proper watering: Water deeply during dry spells, especially young trees. Well-hydrated trees resist infection better.
  • Mulching: A 3-4 inch layer of mulch around the base improves soil health and moisture retention.
  • Pruning: Remove dead, diseased, or crossing branches. Proper pruning improves air circulation and reduces disease pressure.
  • Fertilization: Have your soil tested. Over-fertilizing can actually increase disease susceptibility.
  • Site selection: Plant the right tree in the right place. Trees in shade that prefer sun, or vice versa, are stress-prone.
  • Avoid wounding the tree: Bark damage and pruning wounds are entry points for disease. Use proper techniques.
  • Monitor regularly: Catching problems early is the most important prevention strategy.

When Removal Is the Best Option

Sometimes a tree's disease is too advanced for treatment. A diseased tree can also become a safety hazard — weak branches may fall, or a dying tree may topple in a storm. In Lebanon PA and throughout Lancaster County, when a tree poses a risk to your home, family, or neighboring properties, professional removal is the responsible choice.

Tree removal should be handled only by qualified professionals with proper equipment, insurance, and safety experience. A professional tree removal company will safely fell the tree, remove the debris, and dispose of it responsibly.
