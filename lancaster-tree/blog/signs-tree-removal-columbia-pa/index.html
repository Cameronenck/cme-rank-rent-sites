1. The Tree Is Leaning at an Abnormal Angle

A slight lean is normal in many trees, especially after wind or storm damage. But a significant tilt — especially if the tree suddenly started leaning — is a major red flag.

Why It Matters

A leaning tree often signals compromised roots, soil erosion, or structural failure. Trees in Columbia PA that lean toward your house, shed, or power lines pose an immediate hazard. High winds or heavy snow can send a leaning tree toppling without warning.

What to do: If the lean developed suddenly or worsened after a storm, have a certified arborist inspect it within days, not weeks. The longer you wait, the greater the risk.

2. Large Dead Branches or an Entire Dead Side

Dead branches fall without warning, especially during storms. If you notice:

  • Major branches with no leaves or bark
  • An entire side of the tree that's brown or leafless
  • Brittle branches that snap easily when bent
  • Dead wood throughout the canopy, not just lower branches

...the tree is likely declining rapidly and poses a hazard to people and property below.

Columbia PA Insight

Our Lancaster County area gets heavy snow and ice. Dead branches become projectiles during winter storms. If more than 25% of the tree's canopy is dead, removal is usually the safest option.

3. Significant Trunk Damage or Cavities

The trunk is the tree's backbone. If it's compromised, the tree is in serious trouble:

Watch for These Trunk Issues

  • Large cracks running vertically or horizontally
  • Cavities, hollow sections, or missing bark around the trunk
  • Sunken areas or cankers (dark, diseased sections)
  • Split trunks or major branches breaking away
  • Bark peeling off in sheets

A tree with extensive trunk damage has lost structural integrity. It can't reliably support its own weight or withstand wind and weather.

4. Signs of Disease or Pest Infestation

Some tree diseases and pests can be managed, but others warrant removal:

Most Serious Diseases & Pests in Lancaster County

Emerald Ash Borer

If you have an ash tree in Columbia PA showing D-shaped exit holes, sparse canopy, or thinning branches, it likely has emerald ash borer. Most infected ash trees should be removed within 2-3 years.

Oak Wilt

Red and black oak trees showing sudden leaf discoloration (brown starting at the edges) in mid-summer may have oak wilt. This is progressive and fatal — removal is often necessary to prevent spread.

Anthracnose & Other Fungal Issues

Repeated leaf spotting, premature leaf drop, or branch dieback combined with wet spring weather can indicate fungal disease. If treatment hasn't helped after 2-3 seasons, removal may be needed.

5. Excessive Insect Activity or Oozing Sap

Bark-dwelling insects or unusual sap flow can signal a tree under stress:

  • Oozing amber or reddish sap (often with a foul smell) — sign of stress or disease
  • Carpenter ants, beetles, or borers — especially if you see sawdust-like frass around the trunk
  • Clusters of small insects that keep returning, suggesting the tree is weakened

While some insect pressure is normal, widespread infestation on a tree already showing other decline signs means removal is likely your best option.

6. Roots Are Exposed, Heaving, or Damaging Property

Tree roots should stay in the ground, not create hazards:

Problem Root Signs

  • Large roots heaving up through soil (trip hazard)
  • Roots damaging driveways, patios, or foundations
  • Roots clogging sewer or septic lines
  • Exposed roots indicating soil erosion or root rot

If a tree's roots are actively damaging your property in Columbia PA, you have two choices: contain the roots (sometimes possible) or remove the tree. A certified arborist can evaluate which makes sense.

7. Poor Growth or Bare Branches Despite Being Alive

A tree that's technically alive but barely growing is wasting space and potentially still poses risk:

  • Very sparse canopy with lots of bare branches showing
  • Minimal new growth each season
  • Thin, weak shoots instead of healthy branching
  • Branches dying back from the tips

This tree is stressed and declining. It won't recover. If it's not adding value to your landscape and poses any risk, removal makes sense.

8. The Tree Is Too Close to Your House, Power Lines, or Other Structures

Sometimes a tree doesn't need removal because it's diseased — it needs removal because of where it is. Columbia PA property owners should consider removal if:

  • The tree's canopy directly overhangs your roof
  • Branches are growing against power lines
  • The tree is planted within 10-15 feet of your foundation
  • Roots are threatening a septic field, well, or pool
  • During storms, branches consistently fall toward your home

Professional tree removal keeps your home and family safe. A certified arborist can assess whether the tree should stay (with pruning) or go.

How Much Does Tree Removal Cost?

The cost of tree removal in Lancaster PA depends on several factors:

  • Tree size: Small trees (under 30 feet) typically cost $300-$750; medium trees $750-$2,000; large trees $2,000-$5,000+
  • Location: Trees near structures cost more due to safety precautions
  • Condition: Dead trees are sometimes faster and cheaper to remove
  • Accessibility: Easy access = lower cost
  • Stump removal: Add $200-$500 depending on stump size and grinding

The longer you wait to have a hazardous tree removed, the more expensive it becomes. A tree that's starting to lean or die is easier (and cheaper) to remove than one that has already partially fallen or caused damage.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a leaning tree be saved with cables or braces?

Sometimes. If the lean is from wind damage or a young tree in soft soil, cables can help. But if the roots are compromised or the tree is large and severely tilted, removal is safer and more cost-effective.

Is it safe to remove a dead tree yourself?

No. Dead wood is unpredictable and brittle. Trees of any size should be removed by professionals with proper equipment, insurance, and training. DIY removal causes serious injuries every year.

What if I'm not sure if the tree needs to come down?

Get a professional assessment. A certified arborist can evaluate the tree's health, structural integrity, and risk. Many offer free estimates, and you'll get a clear recommendation.

Can you remove a tree near my Columbia PA home without damaging my property?

Yes. Professional tree removal services use rigging, cranes, and careful dismantling to remove large or dangerous trees safely. We protect your roof, driveway, fence, and landscaping during the entire process.

What should I do after tree removal?

We grind the stump and clean up all debris. You can replant, turn the space into a garden bed, or leave it open. Discuss your plans during the estimate.
