Emergency Tree Service in Lancaster, PA — Available 24/7

Lancaster County gets its share of severe weather — the nor'easters that load every branch with ice, the summer thunderstorms that push 60 mph gusts through the Susquehanna Valley, and the occasional tornado warning that doesn't stay a warning. When a tree comes down at 2 AM on a structure, a vehicle, or a driveway, you need a crew that answers the phone and shows up.

Lancaster Tree Pros provides 24/7 emergency tree service throughout Lancaster County. We're local — Lancaster-based crew, trucks that don't travel from three counties away. When you call 717-297-1693 after hours, you reach us, not a call center.

What Emergency Tree Service Covers

  • Trees on or through roofs, walls, and outbuildings
  • Trees blocking driveways and access roads
  • Trees on vehicles
  • Trees contacting or downing power lines (we coordinate with PPL)
  • Trees with active structural failure — cracked main stems, root heave, split crotches
  • Post-storm hazard assessment — identifying which remaining trees need urgent attention
  • Temporary tarping when structural damage exposes the interior

The Emergency Response Process

When you call, we get the situation — what fell, where, whether there's active utility contact or structural damage. For utility contact, we advise you to call PPL first (1-800-342-5775) and stay clear. We stage near the job and coordinate with utility crews for clearance before we work near live lines.

The priority on arrival is making the situation safe, not making it clean. That means getting the tree off the structure, stabilizing any remaining overhang, and opening the driveway if it's blocked. Full cleanup and remaining stump work often happens on a follow-up visit after the urgent hazard is resolved.

We document everything for insurance purposes. Most homeowner's policies cover storm-related tree removal when a tree has struck a structure. We can provide written documentation of the damage, scope of work, and costs if your carrier requires it.

After the Storm: What Lancaster County Homeowners Should Check

After any major wind or ice event in Lancaster, Lititz, Ephrata, Manheim, or Mount Joy, walk your property and look for these hazards even if no tree came down: large hanging limbs (widow-makers) caught in the canopy that haven't fallen yet, trees showing root heave on the uphill side, bark splits or cracks in the main stem, and leaning that wasn't there before the storm. Call us for a hazard assessment — it's the difference between catching a problem before the next storm and dealing with it after.

Post-storm demand is high. Emergency jobs with active structural damage take priority. Hazard assessment and cleanup work is scheduled as capacity allows, typically within 3–7 days of the storm event.
