WHY PEORIA NEEDS FILTRATION

Limestone Aquifers, Agricultural Runoff, and Aging Infrastructure

Central Illinois tap water has specific challenges that homeowners in other regions do not face. Here is what affects Peoria water quality.

Hard Water from Limestone Aquifers

Central Illinois groundwater passes through thick limestone deposits, picking up calcium and magnesium along the way. This results in hard water that leaves scale on fixtures, shortens appliance life, and makes soap less effective.

Agricultural Runoff Contaminants

Peoria County sits in Illinois farm country. Nitrates, pesticides, and herbicides from surrounding farmland can enter groundwater and wells, particularly after heavy rain. These compounds require specialized filtration to remove.

Aging Pipes and Lead Risk

Many Peoria neighborhoods were built before 1986, when lead solder and lead service lines were common. Illinois American Water has identified distribution lines in the Peoria district that may contribute lead at the tap. Filtering at the point of use protects your household regardless of the source line condition.

Chlorine Taste and Odor

Illinois American Water treats Peoria municipal water with chlorine and chloramine to meet disinfection requirements. While these chemicals are safe at regulated levels, many residents notice the taste and smell in drinking water, ice, and coffee. A carbon filter at the tap removes them completely.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Peoria tap water safe to drink?
Illinois American Water meets federal Safe Drinking Water Act standards, but Peoria's aging distribution lines — some installed before 1950 — can contribute lead and sediment at the tap. A water test is the only way to know what is in your specific home's water.
What causes the taste and smell in Peoria water?
Chlorine and chloramine added during treatment are the most common cause of off-taste and odor in Peoria municipal water. Carbon-based filters remove these effectively at the point of use. Seasonal algae blooms on the Illinois River can also temporarily affect taste.
Do I need a water softener or a water filter?
Many Peoria homes benefit from both. The aquifer water in Central Illinois is naturally hard due to limestone, which causes scale on pipes and appliances. A softener addresses hardness. A filter removes contaminants like chlorine, sediment, or lead. We test first and recommend only what your water actually needs.
How long does installation take?
Most whole-house filter and softener installations are completed in three to five hours. Under-sink reverse osmosis units typically take one to two hours. We schedule around your availability and leave the work area clean.
What does a water filtration system cost in Peoria?
Whole-house filtration systems typically range from $800 to $2,500 installed, depending on the technology and flow rate required for your home size. Water softeners run $900 to $2,200 installed. Reverse osmosis under-sink units are $400 to $900. We provide a clear written estimate before any work starts.
