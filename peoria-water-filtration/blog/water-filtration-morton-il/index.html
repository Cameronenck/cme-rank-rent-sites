Water Filtration in Morton IL: Hard Water Solutions for Tazewell County Homeowners

2026 • Peoria Water Filtration Pros • 13 min read

Morton, IL sits in the heart of Tazewell County, just southeast of Peoria along the Illinois River corridor. The town draws its municipal water primarily from the Illinois-American Water system, which sources from the Illinois River and blends with regional groundwater depending on the season and system demand. It passes federal Safe Drinking Water Act standards before it reaches your tap — but Morton homeowners consistently report the same frustrations: heavy mineral buildup on fixtures, soap that will not lather properly, a faint chlorine taste or odor, and spotty dishes straight out of the dishwasher.

None of these problems mean your water is unsafe. They mean Tazewell County water is hard — genuinely hard — and treating it to legal minimums is not the same as treating it for comfort, efficiency, and long-term appliance health. This guide covers what is actually in Morton tap water, the filtration and softening systems that address each issue, realistic cost ranges, and how to figure out which combination your home actually needs.

What Is Actually in Morton IL Tap Water?

Morton draws from a shared regional water network running through Tazewell County. The Illinois River basin, which supplies a significant portion of this system, carries high mineral loads. Several water quality characteristics are worth understanding before deciding on any treatment system:

Hardness: The Most Expensive Problem You Are Probably Ignoring

Water hardness in the Tazewell County area, including Morton, typically tests in the range of 18 to 26 grains per gallon (GPG). That is solidly in the very hard category — the EPA classification for very hard water begins at about 10.5 GPG, and Morton sits well above that. What that means in practical terms:

  • Calcium and magnesium deposits build up inside water heaters, cutting efficiency and shortening the unit's service life
  • White scale forms on showerheads, faucet aerators, and dishwasher interior walls
  • Soap, shampoo, and detergent perform poorly and leave residue on skin, hair, and laundry
  • Glassware comes out of the dishwasher spotted and filmy even when the machine runs correctly
  • Washing machine drum seals and inlet valves wear faster than they should

Hard water is not a health risk, but it is a significant financial one over time. A water heater scaling up for 10 years operates at meaningfully lower efficiency and fails sooner than one supplied with softened water. A water softener is typically the highest-return investment a Morton homeowner can make in their water system.

Chlorine and Chloramine Treatment

Morton's municipal supply, like virtually all large treated systems in Illinois, uses chlorine or chloramine as a disinfectant to ensure the water stays safe during distribution. This is necessary and legally required — but it is also why many Morton residents notice a flat, slightly chemical taste or a faint bleach smell coming from their taps. Chloramines in particular are stable compounds that persist all the way to the tap and require a specific type of carbon filter (catalytic activated carbon) to remove effectively. Standard pitcher filters and basic carbon blocks do not fully address chloramines.

Disinfection Byproducts

When chlorine interacts with naturally occurring organic matter in source water, it produces regulated compounds called trihalomethanes (THMs) and haloacetic acids (HAAs). These are measured and reported annually in the Consumer Confidence Report for the Morton water system. Levels in Tazewell County generally fall below EPA action thresholds, but they are present in the water supply. Households with young children or pregnant family members often choose to install point-of-use filtration at the kitchen for drinking and cooking water even when system-wide levels remain compliant.

Sediment and Seasonal Variation

Illinois River water sees seasonal turbidity spikes, particularly in spring and early summer when agricultural runoff is highest. Municipal treatment handles the bulk of this, but Morton homes with older service lines or older interior plumbing can still experience fine particulate, rust-colored water, or sediment after pressure changes or nearby main-line work. A whole-house sediment pre-filter at the point of entry addresses this for the entire home before water reaches any fixture or appliance.

Filtration and Softening Options for Morton IL Homes

No single system fixes every water quality issue. The right solution for a Morton home depends on which problems your water actually has and how comprehensively you want to address them. Here are the primary options:

Water Softeners: The Highest-Impact Upgrade for Most Morton Homes

Given the very hard water in Tazewell County, a whole-house water softener is the single highest-return investment most Morton homeowners can make. Traditional salt-based ion exchange softeners work by replacing the calcium and magnesium ions responsible for hardness with sodium ions, eliminating scale throughout the entire plumbing system from the point of entry outward.

A properly sized softener for a typical Morton home — three to four bedrooms, two to three bathrooms, two to four occupants — protects all downstream appliances, eliminates soap scum and spotting, and produces noticeably better results in the shower, laundry, and dishwasher. Professional installation including the unit typically runs $900 to $2,200 depending on unit capacity and plumbing complexity. Ongoing cost is salt, typically $100 to $200 per year for most households.

One important clarification: water softeners address hardness only. They do not filter drinking water contaminants. Most Morton households that install a softener also pair it with a point-of-use drinking water filter at the kitchen for complete coverage.

Under-Sink Reverse Osmosis: Best Drinking Water Option

For the cleanest possible drinking and cooking water in Morton homes, a reverse osmosis (RO) system installed under the kitchen sink is the most effective option available for residential use. RO membranes remove the vast majority of dissolved solids, including lead, nitrates, fluoride, pharmaceutical residues, disinfection byproducts, and essentially anything with a larger molecular weight than water itself. The result is water that is noticeably cleaner-tasting and dramatically lower in total dissolved solids compared to tap.

A quality under-sink RO system includes a sediment pre-filter, a carbon pre-filter for chlorine removal before the membrane, the RO membrane, a post-carbon polishing filter, and a storage tank with a dedicated faucet. System costs run $250 to $600 for quality equipment, with professional installation adding $150 to $300. Annual filter and membrane maintenance runs $80 to $200. This system handles drinking and cooking water only — it is not designed for whole-house supply.

Whole-House Carbon Filtration: Addressing Taste, Odor, and Chloramines

A whole-house carbon filter installed at the point of entry removes chlorine, chloramines, and volatile organic compounds (VOCs) before water reaches any fixture in the Morton home. This is particularly effective for households that notice chlorine odor in showers, the laundry, or the air when running hot water. For chloramine removal specifically, catalytic activated carbon media is required — standard carbon block or granular carbon does not effectively break down chloramines, which are increasingly common in Illinois municipal systems.

Installed cost typically runs $600 to $1,600, with filter media needing replacement every three to five years. Whole-house carbon filtration is often added as a complement to a softener and RO setup for households that want comprehensive coverage across all water uses.

Sediment Pre-Filters: Low-Cost First Line of Defense

For Morton homes with older plumbing, homes near recent main-line work, or homes that notice occasional sediment or discoloration, a whole-house sediment filter at the water meter or main shutoff is a smart low-cost addition. These units protect downstream appliances and filtration systems by catching particulate before it reaches anything else. Installed cost runs $100 to $300, with cartridge replacement needed every three to six months depending on local sediment load.

What the Right Setup Actually Looks Like for Most Morton Homes

After testing water across hundreds of homes in Tazewell County and the broader Peoria metro area, the pattern is consistent. The majority of Morton homeowners see the most benefit from two systems working together: a whole-house water softener to address the hardness that affects every fixture, appliance, and pipe in the house, paired with an under-sink reverse osmosis system at the kitchen for drinking and cooking.

This combination addresses both high-priority water quality issues head-on. The softener eliminates the scale and soap scum that accumulate throughout the entire home and shorten appliance life. The RO system ensures the water your family drinks and cooks with is as clean as it can reasonably be, removing contaminants that even a properly functioning municipal system does not fully eliminate.

For households that notice strong chlorine or chemical odor throughout the house — not just at the kitchen tap — adding a whole-house carbon filter to this combination makes sense. It is a worthwhile addition but typically not the first priority for Morton homes where taste and odor complaints are limited to drinking water rather than whole-home use.

Getting a Water Test Before You Commit

Every Morton home is different. Homes closer to older infrastructure, homes with lead-containing solder in interior plumbing, and homes near agricultural areas can have water quality characteristics that differ from the municipal system average. Before purchasing any filtration or softening equipment, a professional in-home water test gives you the actual numbers for your address — hardness, chlorine and chloramine levels, total dissolved solids, pH, and any contaminants relevant to your specific plumbing.

This matters because an oversized or undersized softener for your actual hardness level and household water usage either wastes salt or fails to protect your plumbing adequately. And a point-of-use filter selected without knowing your actual TDS and contaminant profile may not be the right technology for your water.

Morton IL Water Filtration: Frequently Asked Questions

How hard is the water in Morton IL?

Morton and the surrounding Tazewell County area typically test in the range of 18 to 26 grains per gallon (GPG). That is solidly in the very hard category — the EPA defines very hard water at 10.5 GPG or above. Morton water is roughly two to two-and-a-half times harder than the very hard threshold. A professional in-home water test will give you the exact number for your specific address and water usage.

Do I need a water softener and an RO system, or just one of them?

They serve different purposes. A water softener treats hardness throughout the entire home — every fixture, appliance, and pipe benefits. A reverse osmosis system treats drinking and cooking water at the kitchen only, removing dissolved solids, contaminants, and taste/odor compounds. Most Morton homeowners benefit most from both working together. A softener alone does not clean up drinking water; an RO system alone does nothing for the scale and damage hard water causes throughout the rest of the home.

How much does water softener installation cost in Morton IL?

Most Morton homeowners pay $900 to $2,200 for a water softener system including professional installation. The range reflects differences in unit capacity (sized to your household's daily water usage and hardness level), brand, and plumbing complexity. High-efficiency models on the higher end of the range use significantly less salt and water during regeneration cycles and pay back the price difference over time. See our full guide on water softener installation costs in the Peoria area for a detailed breakdown.

Is Morton tap water safe to drink?

Yes — Morton's municipal water is treated to meet EPA Safe Drinking Water Act standards and is safe to drink straight from the tap. That said, meeting legal minimums is different from delivering the cleanest possible drinking water. Many Morton residents choose to install an under-sink reverse osmosis system for drinking and cooking water to reduce disinfection byproducts, total dissolved solids, and any residual taste or odor compounds not fully addressed by municipal treatment.

Does a water softener remove chlorine and other contaminants?

No. A traditional salt-based water softener addresses hardness minerals only — calcium and magnesium. It does not remove chlorine, chloramines, lead, nitrates, disinfection byproducts, or other dissolved contaminants. For those concerns, you need a carbon filter (for chlorine and chloramines) or a reverse osmosis system (for a broader range of dissolved contaminants). A complete Morton water treatment setup often includes both a softener and at least one additional filtration stage.

How often does a water softener need salt refilled in Morton?

For a typical Morton household of two to four people with hardness in the 18 to 26 GPG range, plan on adding a 40-pound bag of softener salt roughly every four to six weeks. High-efficiency demand-initiated regeneration models use salt more efficiently and can stretch refill intervals longer. Annual salt cost typically runs $100 to $200 for most Morton households. Your installer will walk you through the specific cycle settings and salt usage for your unit's programming.
