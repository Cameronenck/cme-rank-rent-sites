Well Water Filtration for Rural Peoria Area Homes

2026 • Peoria Water Filtration Pros • 7 min read
309-281-3340

Homes on private wells in the Peoria area face a different set of water quality challenges than those on municipal supply. While city water goes through treatment before it reaches your home, well water arrives directly from the aquifer with whatever it picked up along the way. In Central Illinois, that can include a specific combination of minerals and agricultural contaminants worth understanding.

What Is Commonly Found in Peoria Area Well Water

Well water chemistry in Peoria County and surrounding areas reflects the local geology and land use. The most frequently encountered issues in this region include:

  • High hardness from limestone aquifers. Many rural wells in Peoria County test above 25 GPG — harder than typical city water, which has been through some level of treatment.
  • Iron is common in Central Illinois groundwater. Iron above 0.3 mg/L causes rust staining on sinks, toilets, and laundry. Concentrations above 1.0 mg/L also produce a metallic taste.
  • Nitrates from agricultural runoff are a concern in Peoria County and neighboring farmland counties. Nitrate levels above 10 mg/L exceed the EPA maximum contaminant level for drinking water. Infants are particularly vulnerable.
  • Hydrogen sulfide (rotten egg smell) occurs in some wells where sulfur-reducing bacteria are present. It is unpleasant but typically not a health risk at low concentrations.
  • Coliform bacteria should be tested for annually in any private well. Surface contamination, animal waste, or a compromised well casing can introduce bacteria into groundwater.
  • pH imbalance is occasionally found in rural wells. Low pH (acidic water) can corrode copper pipes over time.

Why You Need to Test First

No two wells in the Peoria area test the same. A well in Germantown Hills may have completely different chemistry from a well three miles away in Trivoli. The only way to know what treatment your well water needs is to test it. We perform comprehensive on-site tests that cover hardness, iron, nitrates, pH, hydrogen sulfide, and coliform bacteria, and we can arrange laboratory testing for additional parameters if warranted.

Well Water Treatment Options

Treatment for well water is assembled based on what the test finds:

For high hardness: A water softener handles the same job it does on city water — ion exchange to remove calcium and magnesium.

For iron: Depending on the iron type (dissolved ferrous iron vs. particulate ferric iron), treatment may involve an oxidizing filter, a birm filter, or a greensand filter. Standard softeners remove small amounts of dissolved iron but are not designed as iron filters.

For nitrates: Reverse osmosis is the most effective treatment for nitrates at the drinking water tap. Whole-house nitrate removal is possible with anion exchange systems but is expensive and generally not necessary for non-drinking-water uses.

For hydrogen sulfide: Carbon filters or oxidizing systems eliminate the odor at low concentrations. Higher concentrations may require aeration or advanced oxidation.

For bacteria: UV disinfection systems are the most common solution for private wells. A UV lamp installed on the supply line eliminates bacteria without chemicals.

How Often Should Well Water Be Tested?

Most water quality experts recommend testing private wells annually for basic parameters: bacteria, nitrates, pH, and hardness. If you notice a change in taste, odor, or color, test immediately rather than waiting for the annual schedule. Events like nearby flooding, chemical spills, or new farming activity in the area are also triggers for immediate testing.
