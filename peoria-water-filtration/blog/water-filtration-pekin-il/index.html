Water Filtration in Pekin IL: Hard Water Solutions for Tazewell County Homeowners

Local Water Guide • 2026 • Peoria Water Filtration Pros

What Pekin homeowners need to know about their water supply, why hard water is such a persistent problem in Tazewell County, and which filtration systems actually fix it.

Pekin, Illinois sits just south of Peoria along the Illinois River, and water out of Pekin taps shares the same profile Central Illinois homeowners know well: high mineral content, chlorine taste, and hard water that shortens appliance lifespans and leaves white buildup on every fixture in the house. This guide covers Pekin's water quality picture, the systems that address each issue, and realistic cost ranges for Tazewell County homeowners.

Where Pekin's Water Comes From

Pekin draws municipal water from the Illinois River, treated through the city's water facility to meet EPA Safe Drinking Water Act standards. The treatment removes pathogens and brings regulated contaminants within legal limits — but it does not remove hardness minerals or eliminate chlorine taste and odor. Those characteristics come with the source water and the treatment process itself. Tazewell County water is naturally high in calcium and magnesium, the minerals behind hard water, and that shows up in every Pekin home on city supply.

The Water Quality Issues Pekin Homeowners Actually Face

Hard Water: The Most Expensive Problem

Pekin water hardness typically runs 15 to 22 grains per gallon (GPG) — well into the "very hard" range (above 10.5 GPG). The effects are visible throughout the home: white scale on faucets and showerheads, spots on dishes out of the dishwasher, soap and shampoo that struggle to lather, and scale buildup inside water heaters that reduces efficiency and shortens their lifespan. Hard water is not a health hazard, but over 10 to 15 years it costs Pekin homeowners real money in premature appliance failure and plumbing repairs.

Chlorine Taste and Odor

Pekin uses chlorine or chloramine disinfection, which is necessary but leaves a flat, slightly chemical taste in tap water, coffee, and ice. Chloramines — increasingly common in Illinois municipal systems — are more persistent than free chlorine and require a specific carbon media type to remove effectively at home.

Sediment and Disinfection Byproducts

The Illinois River sees turbidity spikes from agricultural runoff each spring. Most is handled at the treatment plant, but homes with older service lines can still see fine sediment. Chlorine interaction with organic matter in the source water also forms trihalomethanes (THMs) and haloacetic acids (HAAs) — levels are generally within EPA compliance, but households with young children often add point-of-use filtration to reduce long-term exposure.

Water Filtration Options for Pekin IL Homes

Water Softeners

A salt-based ion exchange softener is the single highest-impact investment for most Pekin homes. It removes calcium and magnesium throughout the entire plumbing system, protecting all appliances, eliminating scale and spotting, and improving shower and laundry feel. Installed cost runs $900 to $2,200; ongoing salt use averages $100 to $200 per year. Note: softeners address hardness only — most households also add a point-of-use drinking water filter at the kitchen sink.

Reverse Osmosis Under-Sink Systems

For the cleanest drinking and cooking water, an under-sink RO system removes the vast majority of dissolved solids, including heavy metals, nitrates, fluoride, and disinfection byproducts. A quality system — sediment pre-filter, carbon stage, RO membrane, post-polish filter, dedicated faucet — runs $300 to $650 for equipment plus $150 to $300 for professional installation. Annual filter and membrane replacement costs $80 to $200.

Whole-House Carbon Filtration

For households noticing chlorine odor from showers, laundry, or running hot water, a whole-house carbon filter at the point of entry eliminates that issue at every fixture. Catalytic activated carbon handles chloramine removal effectively. Installed cost is $600 to $1,600, with media replacement every 3 to 5 years. Usually paired with a softener for comprehensive coverage.

Whole-House Sediment Pre-Filters

For older Pekin homes — particularly pre-1975 properties with original galvanized or lead service lines — a sediment pre-filter at the main is a low-cost, meaningful addition. Installed at $120 to $350, with cartridge replacement every 3 to 6 months.

What Most Pekin Homes Actually Need

The pattern across Tazewell County is consistent: the large majority of Pekin homeowners benefit most from a whole-house water softener paired with an under-sink RO system at the kitchen. The softener protects every appliance, pipe, and fixture in the house. The RO system ensures drinking and cooking water is as clean as practically possible. A whole-house carbon filter is a worthwhile addition for households with strong chlorine odor concerns throughout the home. Sediment pre-filters matter most for pre-1980 homes or properties near recent main-line repair work.

Frequently Asked Questions: Water Filtration in Pekin IL

How hard is the water in Pekin IL?

Pekin water is classified as very hard, typically measuring in the range of 15 to 22 grains per gallon (GPG). For context, water above 10.5 GPG is considered very hard, and Pekin consistently falls in that range. The high mineral content comes from both the Illinois River source water and the naturally mineral-rich groundwater throughout Tazewell County. A water softener is the most effective way to address this at the whole-home level.

Is Pekin IL tap water safe to drink?

Pekin's municipal water meets EPA Safe Drinking Water Act standards and is considered safe to drink. The city tests regularly and publishes an annual water quality report. That said, "safe" and "optimal" are different things. Pekin water contains hardness minerals, disinfection residuals (chlorine or chloramines), and measurable levels of disinfection byproducts — all legal and within compliance thresholds, but compounds that many households prefer to reduce through home filtration. A point-of-use reverse osmosis system is the most effective way to improve drinking water quality beyond what the municipal system delivers.

What is the difference between a water softener and a water filter for Pekin homes?

A water softener removes hardness minerals (calcium and magnesium) throughout the entire home, preventing scale buildup on appliances, pipes, and fixtures. It does not filter drinking water contaminants. A water filter — whether whole-house carbon or point-of-use RO — removes chemical contaminants, chlorine, and dissolved solids, but does not address hardness. Most Pekin homeowners benefit from both: a softener for the whole house and an RO system at the kitchen for drinking and cooking water.

How much does a water softener cost to install in Pekin IL?

Installed cost for a salt-based water softener in the Pekin area typically ranges from $900 to $2,200, depending on system size (capacity in grains), brand, and installation complexity. Ongoing costs are primarily salt, which runs $100 to $200 per year depending on household water usage and system efficiency. A properly sized and installed system will last 15 to 20 years with routine maintenance.

Do I need a water test before installing a softener or filter in Pekin?

A water test before purchasing is strongly recommended. Pekin water quality varies somewhat by neighborhood depending on the age of distribution infrastructure and your home's interior plumbing. A professional in-home test measures hardness, pH, total dissolved solids, chlorine, iron, and other relevant parameters specific to your tap. This data is essential for properly sizing a softener — an undersized unit will allow hardness breakthrough, while an oversized one wastes salt and water. We offer a no-cost in-home water test for Pekin homeowners. Call 309-322-7015 to schedule.

Does Pekin IL water have iron in it?

Municipal Pekin water generally has low iron levels at the treatment plant, but iron can enter water through interior plumbing in older homes with galvanized pipes or from the distribution system during pressure events. If you notice orange or rust-colored stains in sinks, toilets, or around drains, that is a sign of elevated iron. A water test will confirm the level. Iron above 0.3 mg/L typically requires an iron-specific filter or a softener with iron-handling capacity — standard softeners can handle low iron levels, but higher concentrations require dedicated iron filtration equipment.
