Water Softener vs. Water Filter: Which Does Your Peoria Home Need?

2026 • Peoria Water Filtration Pros • 6 min read
309-281-3340

Water softeners and water filters are often discussed as if they are competing options, but they serve entirely different purposes. Many Peoria homeowners end up with both because they address different problems in Central Illinois water. Here is a clear breakdown.

What a Water Softener Does

A water softener uses ion exchange to remove hardness minerals — specifically calcium and magnesium — from your water supply. It replaces them with sodium ions, which do not cause scale. The result: no more scale on fixtures, better soap lathering, softer skin and hair, and protection for your pipes and appliances.

A water softener does not filter out chlorine, sediment, lead, nitrates, or other contaminants. Its job is exclusively to remove hardness. Think of it as treating the mineral content of the water, not the chemical or biological safety of the water.

What a Water Filter Does

The term "water filter" covers a range of technologies, but most filters address one or more of the following:

  • Activated carbon filters remove chlorine, chloramine, volatile organic compounds (VOCs), and improve taste and odor.
  • Sediment filters catch physical particles like sand, rust, and dirt.
  • Reverse osmosis systems remove lead, nitrates, arsenic, and most other dissolved contaminants from drinking water at the tap.
  • Iron filters (used on well water) remove dissolved iron that causes rust staining and sulfur odor.

A water filter does not remove hardness. If you have scale on your showerhead and you install only a carbon filter, the scale will continue. You need a softener for that.

The Peoria IL Case for Both

Central Illinois municipal water has two distinct categories of issue that require two distinct solutions:

Hard water from limestone aquifers (15 to 22 GPG in most of Peoria) requires a softener. Chlorine taste, potential lead risk from aging pipes, and sediment from older distribution lines require a filter. These are different problems. A combined approach — softener for hardness, carbon filter for taste and safety — addresses both.

A common installation for Peoria homes looks like this: a whole-house sediment and carbon filter on the main supply line, followed by a water softener. Then, for drinking water specifically, an under-sink reverse osmosis unit provides an additional layer of protection against any trace contaminants.

When You Need Only One

Only a softener: If your water tests show no significant taste/odor problems, no lead concern, and no sediment issues — just high hardness — a softener alone addresses your problem. This is more common in newer Peoria neighborhoods with recently replaced distribution infrastructure.

Only a filter: If you live in an area with naturally soft well water but have taste/odor or sediment concerns, a filter without a softener may be sufficient. This is less common in the Peoria metro area, where hardness is widespread, but applies in some rural areas with specific geology.
