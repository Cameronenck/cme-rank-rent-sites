Reverse Osmosis Water Filter Systems in Peoria IL

A complete guide to RO systems for Peoria homeowners: how reverse osmosis works, what contaminants it removes from Central Illinois tap water, and what installation costs to expect.

What Is a Reverse Osmosis Water Filter?

Reverse osmosis (RO) is a water purification process that pushes tap water through a semi-permeable membrane with pores small enough to block dissolved contaminants that standard carbon filters cannot capture. The result is water that is significantly cleaner than what comes out of the tap in Peoria, East Peoria, Pekin, and most of Central Illinois.

Unlike a basic pitcher filter or refrigerator filter that catches particles and some chlorine taste, an RO system removes dissolved solids, heavy metals, nitrates, fluoride, and a wide range of other contaminants at the molecular level. It is the most comprehensive point-of-use treatment technology available for residential water.

For Peoria homeowners already dealing with hard water or concerned about what Illinois American Water reports find in local tap water, an RO system provides a reliable last line of defense at the kitchen sink or throughout the entire house.

How a Reverse Osmosis System Works

A standard under-sink RO unit runs water through three to five stages before it reaches your glass:

  1. Sediment pre-filter — traps sand, rust, and larger particles that would clog the membrane.
  2. Carbon pre-filter — removes chlorine and chloramines, which would degrade the membrane over time.
  3. RO membrane — the core stage. Water is forced under pressure through a membrane that blocks dissolved solids, heavy metals, nitrates, and most other contaminants. Rejected material is flushed down the drain.
  4. Carbon post-filter (polishing filter) — removes any residual taste or odor before the water reaches your faucet.
  5. Storage tank — clean water is stored under pressure so you have immediate flow at the tap without waiting for the membrane to process each glass in real time.

A tankless RO system skips the storage tank and produces filtered water on demand, which is faster but requires slightly higher water pressure to work efficiently. Both designs are common in Peoria-area homes.

What Reverse Osmosis Removes from Peoria Water

Peoria's municipal water supply comes from the Illinois River and is treated by Illinois American Water before reaching your tap. While that treatment is effective at making water safe under federal standards, it does not eliminate everything homeowners may want removed. An RO system goes further, typically reducing or removing:

  • Total dissolved solids (TDS) — the minerals that cause water to taste flat or chalky
  • Lead and other heavy metals
  • Nitrates and nitrites (a concern in agricultural regions like Central Illinois)
  • Arsenic
  • Fluoride
  • Chlorine and chloramines (added during municipal treatment)
  • Chromium-6
  • PFAS compounds (at high reduction rates with quality membranes)
  • Certain pharmaceuticals and industrial chemicals

An RO system does not soften water in the traditional sense — it removes the calcium and magnesium ions that cause hardness, but it does this through rejection rather than ion exchange. If hardness is your primary concern, a water softener may be a better primary solution, with RO added at the drinking water tap for taste and contaminant reduction.

Under-Sink RO vs. Whole-House RO: Which Is Right for Your Peoria Home?

Most Peoria homeowners install a point-of-use (POU) under-sink RO system that feeds a dedicated faucet at the kitchen sink. This is the most practical and cost-effective approach for drinking and cooking water. Whole-house RO systems are available but are significantly more expensive and produce a large volume of wastewater, which makes them less common in residential settings.

Reverse Osmosis System Cost in Peoria IL

RO system pricing in Central Illinois varies based on system size, brand, number of filtration stages, and whether you hire a plumber or water treatment specialist for installation.

DIY under-sink RO system: Quality five-stage units typically range from $150 to $400 for the unit itself. Installation by a professional plumber in the Peoria area generally adds $100 to $250 in labor.

Professionally supplied and installed under-sink RO: Expect $400 to $900 all-in for a reputable system. Higher-end tankless models with remineralization filters run toward the top of that range.

Annual maintenance: Budget $50 to $120 per year for replacement pre-filters and post-filters. The RO membrane itself typically lasts two to three years and costs $40 to $80 to replace.

Compared to ongoing bottled water purchases, most Peoria households recover the upfront cost of an RO system within one to two years. A family spending $30 to $50 per month on bottled water will typically break even on a well-chosen under-sink system within 12 to 18 months. For a deeper cost comparison, see our guide on bottled water vs. home filtration for Peoria homes.

Is a Reverse Osmosis System Right for Your Peoria Home?

An RO system makes the most sense for Peoria area homeowners who:

  • Are concerned about specific contaminants found in local water quality reports
  • Have well water with high nitrate or heavy metal levels
  • Prefer not to purchase bottled water but want drinking water quality beyond what a basic filter delivers
  • Have household members with health conditions that make water quality especially important
  • Cook frequently and notice tap water affecting the taste of food, coffee, or tea

An RO system is not necessary for everyone. If your main complaint is hard water causing scale buildup on fixtures and appliances, a water softener installation is a more targeted and lower-cost solution. If you want both — soft water throughout the home and the cleanest possible drinking water — the combination of a whole-house softener plus an under-sink RO unit is what most water treatment specialists recommend for Bloomington, Normal, and Peoria IL households.

A free water test is the most useful first step. Without knowing your actual water quality, it is difficult to know whether you need an RO system, a softener, both, or neither.

Reverse Osmosis FAQ for Peoria IL Homeowners

How long does an RO membrane last in a Peoria home?

Most RO membranes last two to three years under normal residential use. Peoria's water contains moderate hardness and chloramine disinfection byproducts, which are why the carbon pre-filter stage matters — it protects the membrane from chlorine degradation. If you notice a drop in water flow or a change in taste, it is usually the pre-filters that need replacing first, not the membrane itself. Annual pre-filter changes are the single most important maintenance step.

Does reverse osmosis remove fluoride from Peoria tap water?

Yes. A quality RO membrane removes 90 to 95 percent of fluoride. Peoria's municipal water supply includes added fluoride as part of public health practice. If you prefer to minimize fluoride in your drinking water, an RO system is one of the most effective residential options available. If you have specific health concerns, consult your physician about what level of fluoride reduction makes sense for your household.

Will RO water taste flat or empty?

RO water is lower in total dissolved solids than tap water, which some people describe as a cleaner or lighter taste. Some homeowners prefer this; others find it tastes flat compared to mineral water. If you want to add minerals back in, remineralization post-filters are available that reintroduce calcium and magnesium at controlled levels. This is a personal preference decision rather than a health one for most households in the Peoria area.

Can I install an RO system myself in my Peoria home?

Many under-sink RO systems are sold as DIY kits and can be installed in two to three hours by a homeowner comfortable with basic plumbing. The connection points are typically the cold water supply line under the sink and the drain line. That said, improper installation can lead to slow leaks inside cabinets. A water treatment professional in Peoria or East Peoria can install a standard system in under an hour and ensure connections are correct and the system is performing as expected before they leave.

Do I need a water softener if I have an RO system?

It depends on your goals. An RO system will remove the calcium and magnesium that cause hardness from the water it treats, but it only covers the kitchen drinking and cooking tap. Your showers, laundry, dishwasher, and water heater continue to receive unsoftened water. In Peoria, where hardness levels are high enough to cause scale buildup on appliances and fixtures, most homeowners benefit from whole-house softening in addition to an RO unit at the kitchen sink. See our softener vs. filter guide for more on how to decide what combination makes sense.

How much water does a reverse osmosis system waste?

Older RO systems can reject two to four gallons of wastewater for every gallon of filtered water produced. Newer high-efficiency and tankless designs have significantly improved this ratio, with some producing close to a 1:1 ratio. For most households in Peoria, the additional water use is modest compared to other household activities and the small increase in water bill is often less than what they were spending on bottled water.
