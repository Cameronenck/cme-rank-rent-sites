Why Traditional Sales Fall Short When You're Relocating

1. Listing Takes Time

Traditional home sales require photography, staging, open houses, and weeks of marketing before offers come in. If you need to sell in 30 days, you're already at a disadvantage. Listing also costs commission (typically 5–6%), which cuts into your proceeds.

2. Buyer Financing Creates Uncertainty

Most traditional buyers need mortgage financing, which introduces appraisal contingencies, inspection contingencies, and lender delays. Your South Bend home could fall out of contract over a low appraisal or a lender's surprise requirement. Meanwhile, your moving truck is already booked.

3. Inspection Repairs Are Unexpected Costs

When a buyer's inspector finds issues, you're forced to negotiate repairs or credits—often delaying closing by weeks. These negotiations can derail deals entirely, leaving you back at square one.

4. You're Still Liable During the Sale

While your home is on the market, you're responsible for property taxes, utilities, maintenance, and HOA fees. If the sale lingers, these carrying costs add up fast, eating into your net proceeds.

The Reality: Even in a favorable market, relocation sellers often accept below-market offers just to close on time. A quick sale at 90% market value beats a "perfect" price that arrives 90 days after you've already moved.
