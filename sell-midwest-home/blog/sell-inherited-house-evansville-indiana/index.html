How to Sell an Inherited House in Evansville, Indiana

August 2026

Inheriting a house in Evansville, Indiana is a blessing, but selling it comes with legal and financial decisions that can feel overwhelming. Whether the property is located in Evansville, Terre Haute, or anywhere in southwestern Indiana, you'll face probate timelines, back taxes, and the choice between listing or accepting a cash offer.

This guide walks you through the reality of selling an inherited property in Indiana and shows you why many Evansville heirs choose cash offers over traditional sales.

Section 1: What Happens to an Inherited Property in Indiana?

In Indiana, when a homeowner dies, their property becomes part of their estate. If there's a will, it goes through probate court in the county where the property sits. The probate process in Indiana typically takes 6 to 12 months, sometimes longer if there are disputes among heirs.

During probate, the property is frozen. You can't sell it without court approval. Meanwhile, you're responsible for:

  • Property taxes (due even while in probate)
  • Homeowner's insurance
  • Maintenance and repairs
  • Utility bills if anyone is living there

If the house is in poor condition or located in a less-active market like Evansville, these carrying costs add up fast. Many Evansville heirs find themselves waiting a year or more while paying thousands in expenses.

Section 2: Probate Timelines in Evansville and Southwest Indiana

Evansville is in Vanderburgh County. Here's what to expect:

  • Simple estates with a will: 6–9 months
  • Estates without a will (intestate): 8–12 months
  • Contested estates: 12+ months (can extend years)
  • Early sale request: 1–3 months if you petition the court

Even with an early sale request, you'll need court permission to list or accept an offer. The executor (or administrator if there's no will) has to file paperwork, notify all heirs, and wait for a hearing.

Section 3: Financial Obligations as an Heir

Just because you inherited the house doesn't mean you inherit it free and clear. The estate must pay:

  • Estate taxes: Federal estate tax applies to estates over $13.61 million (2024). Most Evansville homes won't trigger this, but Indiana doesn't have a state estate tax.
  • Probate court fees: Filing fees, bond fees, and hearing costs (varies by county, typically $500–$2,000)
  • Attorney fees: If you hire a probate lawyer (many do), expect $1,000–$5,000 depending on complexity
  • Back taxes: If the deceased owed property taxes, the estate pays those before distribution
  • Mortgage (if any): The estate must clear the lien before you can inherit clear title

These costs come out of the estate. If the house is the estate's only valuable asset, they can drain a significant portion of what heirs receive.

Section 4: The Choice: Traditional Sale vs. Cash Offer in Evansville

Once you have legal authority to sell (usually after probate closes or with court approval), you have two main paths:

Traditional Real Estate Sale (MLS)

Listing on the MLS in Evansville gives you access to all buyer agents. Typical timeline:

  • List the property: 1–2 weeks to prepare, stage, and photograph
  • Market the home: 30–90 days on market (Evansville market is slower than larger metros)
  • Inspection and negotiation: 30–45 days
  • Close: 30–45 days after acceptance

Total: 4–6 months. And that's the best-case scenario. Older homes in Evansville often sit longer because the buyer pool is smaller. You'll also pay 5–6% commission to agents (split between buyer's and seller's agent).

Cash Offer (Fast Sale)

A cash buyer makes an offer on the inherited property, as-is, without inspection contingencies. Timeline:

  • Offer and acceptance: 1–7 days
  • Due diligence: 7–14 days
  • Close: 14–30 days after due diligence

Total: 3–4 weeks. No contingencies, no repairs required, no staging. You get a number, accept it, and close.

Section 5: Why Evansville Heirs Choose Cash Offers

Evansville's real estate market is stable but not hot. The median home price is lower than Indianapolis or Louisville, and buyer demand is steady rather than competitive. For heirs with inherited properties, cash offers make sense because:

  • Speed matters. Stop paying property tax and insurance while the house sits on the market. A 3-week close beats a 4-month listing.
  • No repair surprises. Older Evansville homes often need work. With a cash buyer, you sell as-is. No inspector finding roof issues that tank the sale.
  • No carrying costs. Even a 1% difference in the offer is worth it if it means skipping 6 months of taxes, insurance, and utilities.
  • Certainty. Cash offers close. No "buyer got cold feet" or "financing fell through" drama. You know the exact close date and amount.
  • Probate complexity solved. Some cash buyers work with estates and will accept an offer contingent on court approval, simplifying the process.

Section 6: Steps to Sell Your Inherited House in Evansville

  1. Get a probate attorney. Have them file the paperwork needed to authorize you (or the executor) to sell. In Evansville, this costs $1,000–$2,500 for a straightforward estate.
  2. Understand the title situation. Is there a mortgage? Back taxes? Liens? Your attorney should title search the property before you market it. In Evansville, a title search costs $100–$300.
  3. Decide: list or sell for cash. If you need certainty and speed, cash is faster. If you can wait 6+ months and want maximum market exposure, list it.
  4. Get the property inspected (optional). If listing, a pre-listing inspection shows buyers the condition. If selling for cash, your buyer will inspect, so this step is less critical. A home inspection in Evansville runs $250–$400.
  5. Market and accept an offer. With cash, this is 1–7 days. With MLS, 30–90 days or more.
  6. Close and distribute funds. After closing, proceeds pay liens, taxes, probate costs, and then heirs receive their share.

Section 7: Tax Implications for Heirs

Good news: inherited property gets a "step-up in basis." That means if your parent bought the house in 1985 for $40,000 and it's worth $150,000 when they die, your cost basis is $150,000. If you sell it a month later for $150,000, you owe $0 in capital gains tax.

This is a massive tax break for heirs. It means you can inherit a property that appreciated significantly and sell it immediately with no tax consequence. Talk to a CPA or tax attorney about your specific situation, but the step-up is standard federal law.

Section 8: Common Mistakes Heirs Make

  • Signing probate papers without legal review. A probate attorney ($1,000–$2,500) saves you from costly mistakes.
  • Delaying the decision. Every month you wait costs money in taxes and insurance. If you're going to sell, do it soon.
  • Listing in a slow market. Evansville homes don't sell as fast as major metros. A 90-day listing in Evansville means real holding costs, not just a "we-get-it" situation.
  • Not getting a title search first. You can't close until liens and back taxes are satisfied. Know these upfront.
  • Assuming fair market value equals what heirs receive. Probate costs, liens, and taxes come out first. If the house sells for $100,000, heirs might see $70,000–$85,000 after expenses.

Section 9: How Evansville Property Values Work

Evansville's real estate market is local. Recent sales, property condition, and neighborhood matter. If your inherited house is in a stable residential area, it'll appraise fairly. If it needs significant work or is in a transitional area, appraisals may come in lower.

A professional appraiser (cost: $400–$600) gives you a realistic value before you list or accept a cash offer. Many cash buyers use this as a starting point for their offers.

Section 10: Probate vs. Non-Probate Transfer

Some inherited properties transfer without probate if they were in a trust or had a beneficiary deed. If your inherited house falls into this category, you might bypass the court process entirely. Check with the executor or a probate attorney to confirm.

Non-probate transfers are faster but still require clear title and resolution of any liens or taxes before sale.

Section 11: Timeline: Cash Offer vs. MLS in Evansville

Cash offer: Probate approval (if needed) → Offer and acceptance (1–7 days) → Due diligence (7–14 days) → Close (14–30 days) = 3–6 weeks post-probate.

MLS listing: Probate approval → Prep and list (1–2 weeks) → Market (30–90+ days in Evansville) → Inspection and negotiation (30–45 days) → Close (30–45 days) = 4–6 months post-probate.

The time difference adds up. So do the carrying costs.
