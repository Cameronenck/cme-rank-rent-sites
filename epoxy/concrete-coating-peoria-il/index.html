Epoxy Isn't the Only Option

Epoxy is the category people know, but polyurea and polyaspartic coatings have become the preferred choice for many applications — especially where faster cure time, UV stability, and greater flexibility matter. Understanding the differences helps you make a better decision for your specific floor.

Epoxy vs. Polyurea vs. Polyaspartic

These three products are often lumped together, but they behave differently on the floor:

  • Epoxy — the traditional foundation. Two-part chemistry that creates a hard, chemically resistant surface. Relatively slow to cure (24 to 72 hours before vehicle traffic), and older epoxy formulations yellow under UV exposure. Best used as a base coat in a layered system, with a UV-stable topcoat protecting it.
  • Polyurea — an evolution of epoxy chemistry. Extremely fast cure (sometimes 60 to 90 minutes before foot traffic), highly flexible, excellent adhesion, and UV stable in its pure form. Used in some one-day floor systems. Higher material cost, but the faster cure reduces labor time significantly.
  • Polyaspartic — a subset of polyurea chemistry, specifically formulated for floor coating applications. Combines the UV stability and fast cure of polyurea with the workability of epoxy. Used almost universally as a topcoat over epoxy base coats. What most modern high-quality floor systems use as the final layer.

For most residential garage and basement projects in the Peoria area, the best system is an epoxy base coat for penetration and adhesion, a flake broadcast for aesthetics and texture, and a polyaspartic topcoat for UV stability and hardness. That's the combination that delivers the longest service life.

One-Day Floor Systems

The main argument for a pure polyurea or polyaspartic system — rather than a traditional epoxy base — is installation time. Because polyurea cures so quickly, a properly equipped crew can prime, coat, broadcast, and topcoat a residential garage in a single day with the floor ready for vehicles within 24 hours. This is appealing for homeowners in Dunlap and Washington who don't want to be without their garage for multiple days.

The trade-off is that faster-cure systems can be less forgiving for the installer — they require experienced application technique and often cost more per square foot than traditional epoxy systems. When done well, one-day polyaspartic systems are excellent. When applied by inexperienced crews who bought a package deal, the results are inconsistent.

Exterior Concrete Coatings

Most epoxy formulations are not suitable for exterior use because they yellow and break down under UV exposure. Polyaspartic coatings are UV stable and can be applied to exterior concrete surfaces — patios, pool decks, walkways, and covered driveways. An exterior coating provides the same benefits as interior epoxy: chemical resistance, easier cleaning, and a polished appearance. In Central Illinois, exterior coatings also help protect concrete from freeze-thaw scaling and the road salt tracked in from driveways.

Concrete Staining vs. Concrete Coating

Concrete stain penetrates the surface and reacts chemically with the concrete to add translucent color. It doesn't add a physical layer — the surface texture of the concrete shows through. Concrete coating sits on top of the concrete and creates a new surface layer. Staining is typically less expensive and better for exterior applications where film-forming coatings can peel. Coating provides more complete protection, a wider range of finish options, and better moisture resistance. Most residential and commercial applications in Peoria benefit more from coating than staining.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the difference between epoxy and polyurea?

Epoxy cures through a chemical reaction that takes 24 to 72 hours and is not UV stable on its own. Polyurea is a faster-curing, UV-stable formulation that is more flexible and more resistant to temperature extremes. Most modern floor systems use epoxy as a penetrating base coat and polyaspartic (a type of polyurea) as the final topcoat to get the best of both.

Can you coat an outdoor patio in Peoria?

Yes, with a UV-stable polyaspartic or polyurea product. Standard epoxy is not suitable for exterior use because it yellows and breaks down under sunlight. The polyaspartic formulations we use for exterior concrete are UV stable, freeze-thaw resistant, and can handle the temperature swings typical of Central Illinois.

How long does polyaspartic take to cure?

Fast-cure polyaspartic formulations allow foot traffic in 6 to 8 hours and vehicle traffic within 24 hours in most conditions. Full chemical cure takes 72 hours to 7 days. Temperature and humidity affect cure time — installations done in cold or damp conditions take longer.

Does concrete coating work on driveways in Peoria winters?

Covered or sheltered driveways, yes. Open driveways exposed to road salt, freeze-thaw cycling, and snowplow contact are more challenging — the edge and joint areas take the most stress. We can discuss the right product and specification for an exterior driveway based on your specific conditions and expectations.

Is there a coating that's safe for use around a pool?

Yes. Non-slip polyaspartic coatings with anti-skid aggregate are specifically designed for pool deck applications. They handle wet foot traffic, resist pool chemicals, and stay cool enough underfoot in summer. We spec the slip-resistance rating based on the expected traffic type and moisture exposure.
