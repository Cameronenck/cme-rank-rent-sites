Commercial Floors Have Different Demands

A garage floor sees two vehicles and the occasional oil drip. A commercial floor sees forklifts, pallet jacks, chemical spills, constant foot traffic, and cleaning chemicals — often around the clock. The coating system that works in a residential garage isn't specified correctly for that kind of loading. Commercial epoxy is a different product category: thicker, more chemically resistant, formulated for high-build applications, and selected based on what the specific floor will actually encounter.

We've installed commercial epoxy floors in warehouses and distribution facilities in the East Peoria industrial corridor, retail and showroom spaces in Peoria proper, food service and restaurant kitchens in Bartonville, and light manufacturing facilities in Washington IL. Each application had a different spec because each floor had a different set of demands.

Choosing the Right Commercial System

The two main decisions in commercial epoxy are system thickness and topcoat chemistry:

  • Standard broadcast system (100 mil) — appropriate for showrooms, retail, office spaces, and light commercial environments. Decorative flake broadcast, polyaspartic topcoat. Looks polished, holds up under foot traffic and occasional cart loads.
  • Heavy-duty industrial system (125–250 mil) — designed for forklift traffic, pallet jack loads, and chemical exposure. Solvent-based epoxy base coats, reinforced with quartz or silica aggregate for hardness and slip resistance. Often left as a flat finish rather than decorative.
  • Antimicrobial / food-safe systems — used in food service, food processing, and healthcare environments. Specific topcoat chemistry provides a surface that resists microbial growth, meets NSF requirements, and can handle the high-pressure cleaning and sanitizing chemicals used in commercial kitchens.
  • Anti-static (ESD) systems — required in electronics manufacturing and certain industrial environments where static discharge is a hazard.

Scheduling Around Your Business

Most commercial clients in the Peoria area can't shut down for two or three days while a floor cures. We work in sections to keep parts of the facility operational, schedule overnight and weekend installations to minimize disruption, and use fast-cure polyaspartic topcoats that allow foot traffic in as little as 6 to 8 hours. For facilities in Washington and Morton where shift schedules allow a defined window, we can plan an installation that has the floor back in service before the next shift starts.

Warehouse Striping and Line Marking

After the base coating is installed, we can apply traffic marking paint or apply precut vinyl striping for pedestrian lanes, forklift paths, rack aisle designations, and hazard marking. Striping over a fresh epoxy floor is cleaner and more durable than striping applied to bare concrete, and it makes OSHA compliance for traffic management significantly easier to achieve and maintain.

Maintenance and Longevity

Commercial epoxy floors are designed to be maintained. A basic maintenance program — regular sweeping to remove abrasive particles, periodic mopping with appropriate pH-neutral cleaners, and prompt cleanup of chemical spills — can extend a commercial floor's service life well beyond 10 years. For high-traffic areas, a topcoat refresh can often be applied over the existing system without full removal, significantly reducing the cost of maintaining the floor over time.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you work nights or weekends to avoid disrupting my business?

Yes. Most of our commercial work in Peoria happens outside normal business hours. We schedule around your operations, not the other way around. Fast-cure polyaspartic topcoats let us complete sections overnight and have them ready for foot traffic by morning.

How quickly can a commercial floor return to service?

Foot traffic typically within 6 to 8 hours with polyaspartic topcoats. Light vehicle traffic (cars, small carts) within 24 hours. Forklift and heavy vehicle traffic typically after 72 hours. We'll give you exact timeline expectations based on the system specified for your project.

What about forklift traffic? Can epoxy handle that?

Industrial-spec epoxy systems are designed for forklift traffic. The key is correct specification: system thickness, aggregate type in the body coat, and topcoat hardness all need to match the actual load and traffic pattern. We specify based on what the floor will see, not a generic commercial product.

Do you do warehouse floor striping?

Yes. Traffic lane striping, rack aisle marking, pedestrian zones, and hazard marking can all be applied over the cured epoxy base system. Striping over epoxy is more durable and sharper-looking than striping applied directly to concrete.

What if I need a food-safe floor for a commercial kitchen?

We install NSF-compliant epoxy systems for food service environments. These use specific topcoat chemistry that resists microbial growth, tolerates high-pressure cleaning, and can handle sanitizing chemicals. Kitchen floors also need to address slip resistance — we spec the appropriate anti-slip aggregate for the application.
