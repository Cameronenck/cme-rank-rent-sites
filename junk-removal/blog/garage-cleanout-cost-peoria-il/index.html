What Affects the Price of a Garage Cleanout

Size of the space. A single-car garage takes less time and fewer trips than a double-car garage stuffed to the ceiling. Square footage matters, but so does how tightly everything is packed.

Type and weight of items. A garage with light boxes and plastic storage bins costs less than one filled with old car parts, concrete blocks, or metal filing cabinets. Heavy items require more labor and sometimes a second trip.

How much you're keeping. If you're selling items on the side or donating certain things, that sorting takes time. We can coordinate donations to local Peoria charities or help you list items for sale, but it extends the job. Pure haul-away jobs move faster.

Accessibility and location. A garage on ground level with easy access is straightforward. If your garage is on a slope, uphill, or blocked by a parked car, it adds time. We'll work around it, but let us know upfront.

Hazardous materials. If the garage contains old paint, batteries, oil, or other hazmat, disposal costs more. Most homeowners don't know what's hazmat—we'll identify it and let you know the additional cost upfront.

How Can I Save Money on a Garage Cleanout?

Sort items yourself ahead of time. Spend a weekend going through everything and marking what's junk, what's trash, and what you might sell or donate. When we arrive, we just load and haul. This saves time and cost—usually cuts 20-30% off the total.

Sell items before we come. Host a garage sale a week or two before scheduling us. Remove items you can get money for, and we haul the leftovers. You keep the sale proceeds and pay less for junk removal.

Donate instead of disposing. We work with Peoria-area charities (Goodwill, Salvation Army, habitat for Humanity ReStore) to donate furniture and decent items. It's often the same price as disposal, but you get the tax write-off.

Bundle with other junk removal jobs. Cleaning out the garage plus clearing the yard, basement, or house? One job, one trip, one fee. Bundling saves you 10-15% per job.

Schedule mid-week, mid-morning. Tuesday–Thursday, 9 AM–2 PM is our slowest window. We sometimes offer same-week discounts for off-peak scheduling.

What Items Are Commonly Removed from Garages in Peoria?

Over the years we've cleared out nearly every type of item you can imagine from Peoria-area garages. The most common items we remove include:

Furniture and appliances: Old refrigerators, freezers, washers and dryers, couches, mattresses, dressers, and dining sets frequently end up in garages "temporarily" — and stay for years. These are heavy and awkward to move alone. We handle them safely.

Tires: Old tires are one of the most common garage finds. Most landfills won't accept them loose, and tire disposal carries extra fees. We handle proper disposal so you don't have to figure it out.

Paint cans: Dried-out or expired paint can't go in the regular trash in Illinois. We identify which paint is still liquid (and may need hazmat disposal) versus dried and safe for disposal. You don't have to guess.

Construction debris: Leftover tile, lumber, drywall, and concrete blocks from home improvement projects. These fill up fast and are heavy to move. We load and haul all of it.

Power tools and hardware: Broken or obsolete tools, old lawnmowers, weed trimmers, snow blowers — anything mechanical that no longer runs. We separate what can be recycled (metal) from what needs disposal.

Boxes and miscellaneous junk: The catch-all category. Old holiday decorations, sports equipment, kids' toys, electronics, cardboard — the accumulated odds and ends that fill the back corners of every garage.

Recyclable vs. Landfill: How We Sort

One of the benefits of hiring professionals is responsible disposal. We separate your garage cleanout debris into categories:

Recyclable metals — old tools, hardware, appliance parts, and metal furniture go to Peoria-area scrap facilities rather than the landfill.

Donatable items — furniture and household goods in usable condition are flagged for donation to Goodwill, Salvation Army, or Habitat for Humanity ReStore in the Peoria area.

Electronics (e-waste) — old TVs, computers, and electronics require separate handling under Illinois e-waste rules. We sort these and bring them to certified e-waste facilities.

Hazardous material — paint, batteries, oils, and chemicals that can't go to a standard facility are identified and handled through proper disposal channels. We'll let you know upfront if anything in your garage requires hazmat disposal, which may carry a small additional fee.
