5 Signs Your Garage Door Spring Is About to Break

July 2026 • Lancaster Garage Door Pros • 5 min read

A garage door spring doesn't usually fail with zero warning. There are signs — sounds, behaviors, and visual cues — that show up in the weeks or months before a spring snaps. If you catch them early, you schedule a repair on your terms instead of calling for emergency service on a Tuesday morning when you can't get your car out.

Here are the five things Lancaster County homeowners should watch for.

01

The Door Is Slower Than It Used to Be

A spring's job is to counterbalance the door's weight so the opener motor isn't doing all the work. When a spring starts losing tension, the opener has to work harder. You'll notice the door opening and closing more slowly, or the motor straining audibly. Some smart openers will log increased motor amperage. If your door has always zipped open in about 10–12 seconds and it's now taking 16–20 seconds, the spring is a likely cause.

02

The Door Doesn't Stay Open When You Disconnect the Opener

Here's a simple test: disconnect the opener (pull the red emergency release cord) and try to open the door manually to waist height. A properly tensioned door should stay in place when you let go. If it falls back down on its own, the spring has lost tension and is close to failing. A properly sprung door should feel nearly weightless to lift manually. If it feels heavy, the spring is weak.

03

You Hear Squeaking, Grinding, or a Metallic Creaking When the Door Moves

Some noise from a garage door is normal — chains and rollers make sound. But a new squeaking or metallic creaking that wasn't there before often comes from a spring under uneven tension. When one side of a two-spring torsion bar system loses tension faster than the other, the door binds slightly in the tracks. You'll hear it as a rhythmic creak or scrape as the door moves. Don't ignore new sounds — they mean something mechanical is changing.

04

Visible Gaps or Separation in the Spring Coil

Look at the torsion spring above your door (the horizontal spring mounted on the metal shaft). If you can see a gap — a section where the coils are spread apart rather than tightly wound — that's a failing or already-failed spring. Extension springs (the ones that run along the sides of the door) can show visible stretching or uneven coil spacing. Either way, this is beyond a "watch it" situation. A spring showing physical separation should be replaced immediately.

05

The Door Looks Crooked When Opening or Has an Uneven Gap at the Top

If one side of the door is rising faster than the other, or you notice the door looks tilted in its frame, one spring has likely already failed or lost significantly more tension than the other. Doors with extension springs (one on each side) are particularly prone to this — when one spring fails, the other side can't support the weight evenly. A crooked door is a door that's about to jump its tracks if you keep operating it.

Lancaster County timing note: Spring failures are most common in January and February. Cold steel contracts overnight and if a spring is already stressed, the temperature drop from 40°F to 10°F is often enough to snap it. If your springs are 7+ years old, a fall inspection is cheap insurance against an emergency call in the middle of winter.

What to Do If You See These Signs

Don't try to replace a torsion spring yourself. They store an enormous amount of energy — enough to cause serious injury if they release unexpectedly. This is one of the few garage door tasks where professional service is genuinely necessary, not just recommended.
