What Can't Be Thrown in the Trash in Peoria IL — Disposal Rules Explained

Published October 6, 2026 · Peoria Junk Removal Pros

Home › Blog › What Can't Be Thrown in the Trash in Peoria IL

Every week someone in Peoria loads up a trash bin with something that is not supposed to be there — a half-full paint can, a dead refrigerator, a box of old batteries, a mattress leaning against the curb. Sometimes it gets picked up anyway. Often it does not, and it sits there until the homeowner figures out what to do with it. The rules around what can and cannot go in regular trash are real, they vary by material, and ignoring them can mean a fine, a refused pickup, or a safety problem for the crew. Here is a plain-language guide to what cannot go to the curb in Peoria IL, why, and how to get rid of it the right way.

Why Some Items Cannot Go in the Trash

Regular household trash in Peoria goes to a transfer station and then to a landfill, and that system is built to handle everyday waste — food scraps, packaging, paper, broken small items. It is not built to handle anything hazardous, anything with refrigerant, anything that can catch fire, or anything that is simply too big for the truck. When those items get mixed into the regular stream, they can injure workers, damage equipment, contaminate a load, or violate state and federal disposal rules. That is why the list of prohibited items exists, and it is why haulers and municipalities take it seriously.

Household Hazardous Waste — Never in the Bin

This is the category that causes the most confusion. Household hazardous waste includes paint, paint thinner, solvents, stains and varnishes, pesticides and herbicides, fertilizers, pool chemicals, antifreeze, motor oil, and household cleaners of the stronger variety. None of these belong in curbside trash. They need to be taken to a household hazardous waste collection event or a designated drop-off. Pouring them down a drain or into a storm sewer is illegal and harmful — in Peoria, storm drains lead toward the Illinois River, not to a treatment plant.

Paint, Chemicals, and Propane Tanks

Paint deserves its own mention because it is the single most common problem item we see. Latex and oil-based paint cannot go in the trash while liquid. If a can has fully hardened, some programs will accept it with the lid removed, but the reliable path is a hazardous waste event. The same goes for propane tanks, which cannot be thrown away even when empty because they can still hold pressure and are a genuine explosion hazard in a garbage truck. Small camping cylinders and full-size grill tanks both need special handling.

Appliances with Refrigerant

Refrigerators, freezers, air conditioners, and dehumidifiers all contain refrigerant, and federal rules require that refrigerant be recovered before the appliance is scrapped. That means these units cannot go to the curb as ordinary trash. They need a licensed hauler or a scrap processor who will pull the refrigerant properly. This is a big part of what we handle in Peoria — we remove the appliance, manage the recovery, and dispose of the unit so you are not left with a dead fridge in the garage for six months.

Batteries and Electronics

Lithium batteries are a serious fire risk once they are in a collection truck or a landfill. They should never go in curbside trash. Car batteries are also prohibited and need to go to a battery recycler or an auto parts store that accepts them. Electronics are their own category — Illinois restricts landfilling certain electronic devices, so computers, monitors, televisions, and similar items should go to an electronics recycler rather than the bin. Many retailers take small electronics and batteries for free.

Bulk Items: Furniture, Mattresses, and Carpet

A mattress and box spring will almost never be collected as regular trash. Neither will a sectional sofa, a large dresser, or rolled-up carpet. Peoria and the surrounding communities generally require you to schedule a bulk pickup in advance, and many programs charge a separate fee for it. If you have more than one or two bulk items, a hauler is usually cheaper and far less hassle than coordinating multiple bulk collections across several weeks. That is exactly the service we provide.

What CAN Go in Regular Trash

It helps to know the other side of the list. Regular trash is the right place for food scraps and packaging, paper and cardboard, non-hazardous cleaning supplies that are fully used up, broken small household items that are not electronic, and bagged general household waste. If it fits in the bin, is not hazardous, is not electronic, and is not too heavy or bulky for the truck, it is almost certainly fine for the regular curbside stream.

How to Get Rid of What Cannot Go to the Curb

You have three practical options. First, use a household hazardous waste event or a designated drop-off for paint, chemicals, and batteries. Second, schedule a bulk pickup with your municipality for large items. Third, and simplest for most people, call a hauler who handles the whole range at once. We take furniture, appliances, mattresses, yard waste, construction debris, and general clutter, and we tell you upfront if something on your list belongs in the hazardous category instead. You do not have to sort it all out yourself or make three separate trips.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I put paint cans in the trash in Peoria IL?
No. Latex and oil-based paint are considered household hazardous waste and cannot go in your regular curbside bin. If a can is completely dried out, some haulers will accept it with the lid off, but the safest path is to take paint to a household hazardous waste collection event or have it hauled by a service that handles it properly. Never pour paint down a drain or into a storm sewer.
Are mattresses and furniture allowed at the curb in Peoria?
Usually not as regular trash. Bulk items like mattresses, sofas, box springs, and large furniture generally require a scheduled bulk pickup or a trip to the transfer station, and many curbside programs charge extra for them. In Peoria you often need to call ahead and arrange a bulk collection, or hire a hauler to remove them. This is one of the most common reasons people call us.
What do I do with old refrigerators, freezers, and air conditioners?
Refrigerators, freezers, air conditioners, and dehumidifiers contain refrigerant and are regulated as appliances. They cannot go to the curb as ordinary trash. They need to have the refrigerant recovered before disposal, which a licensed hauler handles. We take these appliances and manage the freon recovery so you do not have to.
Can I throw away batteries and electronics in Peoria?
No. Lithium batteries, car batteries, and most electronics should not go in the regular trash. Lithium batteries are a fire risk in collection trucks and at the facility, and Illinois law restricts landfilling certain electronics. Recycle batteries at a designated drop-off and check with a local electronics recycler for computers, TVs, and monitors.
