Spray Foam vs Fiberglass Insulation: What Peoria Homeowners Should Know

Published August 28, 2026 | Peoria, IL

When it comes to insulating your Peoria home, you have options. The two most common types of insulation are spray foam and fiberglass batts, each with distinct advantages and drawbacks. Choosing the right insulation can significantly impact your energy bills, comfort, and home value. In this guide, we'll break down the key differences so you can make an informed decision for your East Peoria or Peoria home.

What is Fiberglass Insulation?

Fiberglass insulation is the traditional choice for most American homes. It comes in batts (pre-cut panels), rolls, or loose-fill form and consists of fine glass fibers bound together with a resin. Fiberglass has been the go-to insulation for decades because it's affordable and relatively easy to install.

Fiberglass is commonly found in attics, walls, and crawl spaces across Peoria, IL, and the surrounding areas. It's a passive insulation material, meaning it simply slows heat transfer without actively sealing air leaks.

What is Spray Foam Insulation?

Spray foam is a liquid insulation that expands and hardens after application. It comes in two types: open-cell (lower density) and closed-cell (higher density). Once sprayed, it fills every cavity, crack, and gap, creating an airtight seal while providing insulation. Unlike fiberglass, spray foam serves a dual purpose: insulation and air sealing.

For Peoria homeowners looking for premium performance, spray foam is often the top choice for attics, crawl spaces, basement rim joists, and above-grade walls.

Key Differences Explained

R-Value and Insulation Performance

Closed-cell spray foam has an R-value of 6.0-7.0 per inch, while open-cell comes in at 3.6 per inch. Fiberglass typically ranges from 3.2-3.8 per inch. This means spray foam needs less thickness to achieve the same R-value as fiberglass. For Peoria homeowners with limited attic or wall space, spray foam is more efficient per inch.

Air Sealing and Energy Loss

The biggest advantage of spray foam is its ability to seal air leaks. Fiberglass is installed in between framing, but air can still flow around the edges of batts, reducing overall effectiveness. In Peoria's climate, where heating and cooling costs are significant, this difference in air sealing translates directly to lower energy bills with spray foam. Studies show homes with spray foam use 15-25% less energy than comparable homes with fiberglass.

Cost Considerations

Fiberglass is significantly cheaper upfront. For a typical Peoria home attic (1,500 sq ft), fiberglass batts cost $600-$1,800, while spray foam runs $2,250-$5,250. However, the higher energy savings from spray foam can recoup the difference in 5-10 years through lower utility bills.

Moisture and Mold Resistance

Closed-cell spray foam is impermeable and resistant to moisture, making it ideal for crawl spaces and basements where moisture is a concern. Fiberglass absorbs moisture readily and can develop mold if exposed to dampness—a real risk in Illinois basements. For Peoria homeowners with any moisture issues, spray foam is the safer choice.

Installation and Labor

Fiberglass batts can be installed by homeowners with basic tools. Spray foam requires licensed professionals with specialized equipment. This adds to the cost but ensures proper installation and safety (closed-cell foam produces high pressure during application).

Longevity and Durability

Spray foam lasts 80+ years without significant degradation. Fiberglass typically lasts 15-25 years before settling and losing effectiveness. In a 30-year home ownership period, you might replace fiberglass twice, further narrowing the cost advantage.

Which Should You Choose for Your Peoria Home?

Choose Spray Foam If:

  • You want maximum energy savings and lower monthly utility bills
  • Your home has moisture or mold issues (especially in crawl spaces or basements)
  • You're insulating areas with irregular shapes or many gaps
  • You plan to stay in your Peoria or East Peoria home long-term
  • You want a solution that will last decades without settling
  • Your budget allows for higher upfront costs

Choose Fiberglass If:

  • Budget is your primary concern
  • You're comfortable with DIY installation
  • Your attic is dry with no moisture concerns
  • You're doing a quick upgrade before selling
  • Your home's framing is standard and uniform

Illinois Energy Efficiency: The Real Difference

Illinois winters are cold, and summers are humid. Fiberglass alone leaves air leaks that allow heating and cooling to escape. Spray foam's complete air seal is specifically designed for Illinois climates. Peoria homeowners who upgrade to spray foam typically see energy bill reductions of 15-25%, paying for the installation within 5-10 years.

Blown-In Insulation: A Middle Ground?

There's a third option: blown-in fiberglass or cellulose. Blown-in insulation offers better coverage than batts because it fills gaps more completely, but it still doesn't provide the air sealing benefits of spray foam. It costs more than fiberglass batts ($1.00-$1.50 per sq ft) but less than spray foam. For Peoria homeowners looking for a compromise, blown-in insulation can be a good middle-ground option, especially for attics where air sealing is less critical.

The Bottom Line for Peoria Homeowners

Spray foam is the premium choice for energy efficiency, durability, and long-term savings. Fiberglass is the budget-friendly option for initial installation. For most Peoria and East Peoria homeowners, the superior performance and longevity of spray foam justify the higher upfront cost. Over a 30-year period, spray foam typically delivers better value when you factor in energy savings and the need to replace fiberglass one or more times.
