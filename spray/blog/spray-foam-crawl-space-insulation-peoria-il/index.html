Why Crawl Spaces Matter in Peoria IL

Peoria's weather is brutal on crawl spaces. Winters drop below freezing for months, and summers bring humidity that sticks around. Your crawl space sits directly below your living space with no barrier between them.

Here's what happens in an uninsulated crawl space:

  • Winter: Cold air sneaks in through foundation cracks and leaks. It rises into your home, so your furnace runs longer and costs more.
  • Summer: Humidity enters through soil, foundation seeps, and outdoor air. It carries mold spores, dust mites, and stale air up into your home and makes cooling harder.
  • Year-round: Moisture collects on any exposed wood or metal, creating conditions for mold, rot, and pests.

If your crawl space has old fiberglass batts, they've probably settled, gotten wet, and lost most of their R-value. The result: you're essentially heating and cooling the outdoors every season.

How Spray Foam Fixes Crawl Space Problems

Closed-cell spray foam insulation creates an air-tight, vapor-sealed barrier. When applied to crawl space walls and the band board (rim joist area), it stops cold drafts, blocks humid air, and eliminates the gaps where moisture collects.

Here's the difference between spray foam and traditional insulation in a crawl space:

Feature Fiberglass Batts Blown-In Fiberglass Closed-Cell Spray Foam
Air sealing Poor — air moves around batts Fair — some settling Excellent — hermetic seal
Moisture resistance Poor — absorbs water Poor — absorbs water Excellent — vapor-impermeable
Mold resistance Poor — sustains mold growth Poor — sustains mold growth Excellent — inert, no food source
Longevity 20-30 years (if dry) 20-30 years (if dry) 50+ years (won't degrade)
R-value per inch 3.2-3.7 3.2-3.7 6.0-7.0 (closed-cell)

The key advantage is that closed-cell spray foam is air-sealing. It fills every crack, corner, and cavity, and it bonds to the concrete foundation. Water and air can't move past it. That's why a crawl space with closed-cell foam stays dry, stays warm in winter, and stays cool in summer.

Crawl Space Spray Foam Application: Walls vs. Floors

This is critical to get right. In a Peoria crawl space, you insulate the walls and band board, not the floor.

Why not the floor? Your crawl space floor sits on soil and collects groundwater. If you insulate the floor with foam (or anything else), you trap moisture between the insulation and the concrete. Over months and years, that water accumulates, and you get mold, rot, and structural damage. It's one of the worst mistakes homeowners make.

The correct approach:

  • Spray closed-cell foam on all crawl space walls, from the top of the foundation to the band board (rim joist).
  • Seal the band board completely — this area is where the most air leakage and heat loss happen.
  • Install a proper vapor barrier (heavy plastic sheeting) on the crawl space floor to block ground moisture.
  • Do not insulate the floor itself.

This creates a sealed, conditioned buffer that prevents moisture from entering your home, stops air infiltration, and keeps your crawl space dry. Your living space above stays warmer in winter, cooler in summer, and free from the musty, damp smell that haunts so many basements and crawl spaces.

Open-Cell vs. Closed-Cell Spray Foam for Crawl Spaces

Some contractors try to save money by using open-cell spray foam in crawl spaces. Don't let them.

Open-cell spray foam: This is semi-permeable. It lets moisture pass through it and actually absorbs water like a sponge. In a crawl space where humidity is constant, open-cell foam will absorb moisture, lose R-value, support mold growth, and eventually break down. It's a short-term fix that costs more to replace than to get it right the first time.

Closed-cell spray foam: This is vapor-impermeable. Water and air can't pass through it. It's inert (doesn't absorb or decay), won't support mold, and stays at full R-value for 50+ years. Closed-cell is the only choice for crawl spaces.

Common Questions About Crawl Space Spray Foam

Q: Will spray foam in my crawl space prevent pests?

A: Spray foam itself doesn't repel pests, but it eliminates the conditions that attract them. Closed-cell foam stops moisture infiltration and air leaks that create humid, inviting environments for insects and rodents. Combined with a proper vapor barrier, it makes crawl spaces inhospitable.

Q: Can I install spray foam myself?

A: No. Spray foam application requires specialized equipment, training, and proper PPE. It also requires building permits in most cases. Professional installation ensures proper thickness, coverage, and code compliance. DIY attempts often result in incomplete coverage, overspray damage, or safety issues.

Q: What if my crawl space has standing water?

A: Standing water needs to be addressed first. We can often install a sump pump or improve drainage, then spray. Addressing water problems first ensures the foam stays effective and your crawl space stays dry long-term.

Q: Is crawl space spray foam noisy or disruptive?

A: The spray application takes 1 to 2 days for most crawl spaces. It's noisy during the actual spraying, but that's usually 4 to 6 hours. Most homeowners leave during the day and return to a completed job.
