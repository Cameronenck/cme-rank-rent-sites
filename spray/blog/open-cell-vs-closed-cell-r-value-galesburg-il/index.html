Open Cell vs Closed Cell Spray Foam R-Value in Galesburg IL

Published: October 6, 2026 | Reading Time: 8 minutes | Peoria Spray Foam

If you own an older home in Galesburg IL, you have probably felt a draft that no amount of caulk seems to fix. Much of Galesburg's housing stock was built before World War II, when attics were vented, walls were left empty, and the idea of an air barrier did not exist.

When homeowners here research spray foam, the first question is almost always the same: open cell or closed cell, and which one has the R-value I actually need? They are different products solving different problems, and R-value per inch is only part of the decision.

What R-Value Actually Measures

R-value measures thermal resistance — how well a material slows heat from moving through it. Higher R-value means slower heat transfer. It says nothing about air leakage, moisture, or structural contribution.

That distinction matters in Galesburg, where the dominant problem in most homes is not thin insulation but air moving through gaps. Fiberglass batts rated at R-13 perform far below that when air passes straight through them. Spray foam closes the gap and insulates in the same step.

Key point: R-value assumes the material is installed perfectly with no air movement. In a real Galesburg attic or rim joist, installation quality and air sealing decide the outcome as much as the number on the label.

R-Value Per Inch: Open Cell vs Closed Cell

This is the headline difference, and it is the number most homeowners look for first.

Property Open Cell Closed Cell
R-value per inch Roughly R-3.5 to R-4 Roughly R-6 to R-7
Cell structure Open, spongy, air-permeable Closed, dense, near-impermeable
Air sealing Good Excellent
Vapor behavior Vapor-open Vapor-retarding at depth
Best use Attics, interior walls, large open cavities Rim joists, crawl spaces, exterior sheathing, metal buildings

In practical terms, closed cell delivers roughly twice the R-value in the same depth. Where open cell needs about three and a half inches to reach R-13, closed cell gets there in roughly two.

Why the Difference Matters in Galesburg and Western Illinois

Western Illinois sees winter design temperatures well below freezing and high summer humidity. That forces two requirements at once: enough R-value to slow heat loss, and a robust air barrier to stop moist air from reaching cold surfaces.

In a vented Galesburg attic, open cell is usually right. It fills the irregular joist bays of an older home, seals air leaks fiberglass missed, and costs less per inch. The attic is forgiving — vapor-open foam lets the assembly dry.

At the rim joist and in an unvented crawl space, closed cell is usually right. Those assemblies sit at the coldest, dampest part of the envelope, and its vapor-retarding character keeps joist ends dry through a Galesburg winter.

How Much R-Value Do You Actually Need?

Illinois follows the residential energy code requirements for its climate zone. Practical targets for a Western Illinois retrofit:

  • Attic: R-49 to R-60 for a vented assembly. With closed cell at the roof deck, common targets land in the R-30 to R-38 range since the assembly is unvented and air-sealed.
  • Walls: R-13 to R-21 in a 2x4 or 2x6 cavity, depending on foam type and sheathing.
  • Rim joists and band areas: R-15 to R-21 with closed cell, limited by the joist dimension.
  • Crawl space walls: R-10 to R-15 with closed cell on the walls rather than the ceiling.

The right target depends on your assembly, not a generic number. For a fuller breakdown, see our guide on spray foam R-value requirements in Normal IL.

The Cost Tradeoff Between the Two Foams

Closed cell costs more per board foot than open cell because it uses more material and a denser formulation. The comparison only makes sense per unit of R-value, not per inch.

  • Open cell: lower material cost, needs more depth for the same R-value, better for large open cavities.
  • Closed cell: higher material cost, reaches the target R-value in less depth, and adds vapor control and some structural rigidity.
  • Hybrid installs: many Galesburg projects use closed cell at the rim joists and crawl space and open cell in the attic.

How to Choose for Your Galesburg Home

  1. Identify the assembly first. Attic, wall, rim joist, and crawl space each have a different answer.
  2. Decide how much depth you have. Limited cavity depth favors closed cell, which reaches the target R-value in fewer inches.
  3. Check the moisture path. Damp crawl spaces and rim joists favor closed cell; dry vented attics favor open cell.
  4. Air seal regardless. Both foams air seal; the difference is R-value per inch and vapor behavior.
  5. Get a written scope. A quote should state the foam type, target thickness, and resulting R-value.

If a contractor quotes "spray foam" without naming the type and thickness, ask for it in writing before signing.

Common Mistakes With Spray Foam R-Value in Western Illinois

  • Using open cell at the rim joist. It air seals, but does not provide the vapor control that assembly needs here.
  • Assuming thickness equals R-value. Six inches of one foam is not six inches of the other. Always convert to R-value.
  • Foaming over wet framing. Foam traps moisture against wood. Fix active water intrusion first.
  • Skipping the air sealing audit. Foam over an unaddressed leak path wastes material and underperforms its rating.

Spray Foam vs. Other Insulation on R-Value

  • Fiberglass batts: rated R-value is real, but performance drops sharply with air movement and imperfect installation.
  • Blown-in cellulose: good coverage in open attics, lower R-value per inch than closed cell, no air sealing.
  • Rigid foam board: predictable R-value, but seams and gaps need separate sealing.
  • Spray foam: highest R-value per inch in the closed cell formulation, plus air sealing in the same pass.
