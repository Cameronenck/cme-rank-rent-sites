How Spray Foam Stops Energy Waste

Unlike traditional insulation, spray foam doesn't just fill spaces—it expands to create an airtight barrier. Here's why it's so effective:

  • Seals air leaks: Spray foam adheres to every surface, eliminating the tiny gaps where fiberglass allows air to escape.
  • Reduces thermal bridging: Wood studs and metal framing conduct heat. Spray foam's continuous coverage minimizes this energy loss.
  • Maintains R-value over time: Fiberglass settles and loses effectiveness. Spray foam maintains its insulating power for decades.
  • Eliminates hot and cold spots: Consistent insulation means no drafts, no temperature fluctuations, and even comfort throughout your Bloomington home.

Where Bloomington Homes Lose the Most Energy

In Bloomington's climate, these areas are energy drains—and spray foam tackles them all:

  • Attic: Heat rises in winter, escapes through the roof. Spray foam seals attic rim joists, rafter tails, and penetrations for maximum attic insulation efficiency.
  • Basement and crawl spaces: Cold floors and drafts from foundation cracks. Spray foam seals the rim joist, covers concrete walls, and eliminates moisture problems that reduce insulation effectiveness.
  • Exterior walls: Air leaks around windows, doors, and wall cavities. Spray foam fills every gap, creating a continuous thermal barrier.
  • Garage: If your garage is attached, air leaks from the garage wall cost you. Spray foam seals the common wall and stops heat loss.

Open-Cell vs Closed-Cell: Which Saves More Energy?

Closed-Cell Spray Foam

Best for maximum energy savings in Bloomington:

  • R-value: 6.5-7.0 per inch (highest)
  • Air sealing: Nearly 100% airtight
  • Moisture resistance: Excellent for basements and crawl spaces
  • Energy savings: 20-35% reduction in heating/cooling costs
  • Cost: $1.50–$2.50 per board foot

Open-Cell Spray Foam

Good option if budget is a priority:

  • R-value: 3.5-4.0 per inch
  • Air sealing: Still very effective, though not as complete as closed-cell
  • Moisture sensitivity: Not ideal for wet areas (dries quickly but not moisture-resistant)
  • Energy savings: 15-25% reduction in heating/cooling costs
  • Cost: $0.55–$1.00 per board foot

For Bloomington homes, closed-cell spray foam delivers the highest ROI for energy savings, especially in attics and basements.

How Spray Foam Insulation Gets Installed

Understanding the process helps you plan and budget:

  1. Energy audit: Professional assessment identifies where your Bloomington home is losing heat and cooling.
  2. Preparation: HVAC ducts, electrical, and plumbing are protected or relocated if needed.
  3. Spray application: Licensed technicians apply foam in layers, allowing it to cure between coats.
  4. Trimming: Excess foam is trimmed flush with framing (required before drywall).
  5. Verification: Inspection confirms proper coverage and R-value achievement.
  6. Follow-up: Drywall or finish materials are installed once foam has fully cured.
