Why Technique Matters

Soft Wash vs. Pressure Wash for Siding

Lancaster County homeowners often ask whether their house needs "power washing" or "pressure washing." The more important question is whether it needs soft washing — and for most siding types, the answer is yes.

High-pressure washing — anything above 500–800 PSI on siding — can cause serious and expensive damage. On vinyl siding, it can crack panels, break seams, and drive water behind the cladding where it sits against the sheathing and breeds mold from the inside out. On wood or painted surfaces, it strips paint, raises the grain, and creates moisture pathways that accelerate rot. On stucco, aggressive pressure can chip the finish coat or open hairline cracks.

Soft washing solves this problem entirely. We apply a biodegradable cleaning solution at low pressure — typically under 150 PSI — that attacks mold, algae, and mildew at the cellular level. The organisms die and release from the surface, then we rinse gently. Because the biology is treated rather than just blasted off, results last significantly longer than a straight pressure wash.

The distinction matters especially in Lancaster County, where humid summers, dense tree canopy in neighborhoods like Lititz and Ephrata, and older housing stock create ideal conditions for algae and mold to establish quickly. A high-pressure wash might look clean for a few months; a proper soft wash keeps your home looking clean for 2–3 years.

What Soft Washing Treats

Our cleaning solution is designed to eliminate the biological organisms that discolor and degrade your siding — not just rinse away surface dust.

  • Gloeocapsa Magma algae (dark streaks)
  • Green algae on shaded surfaces
  • Black and white mold colonies
  • Mildew causing gray discoloration
  • Lichen (treated with follow-up visits)
  • Oxidation on older painted surfaces
  • Airborne dust and pollution film
  • Spider webs, wasp nests, insect residue
The Process

How a House Wash Works in Lancaster

Pre-Wash Inspection and Prep

Before we apply any solution, we walk the exterior of your home and note any damaged siding, gaps in caulking, or open windows that need attention. We pre-wet all plantings — garden beds, shrubs, grass along the foundation — to protect them. Delicate areas get extra rinse cycles before and after the cleaning solution is applied.

Application and Dwell Time

We apply our low-pressure cleaning solution from the bottom up, which prevents streaking. The solution dwells for 5–15 minutes depending on how heavy the biological load is. This dwell time is what makes soft washing effective — the surfactants and oxidizers in the solution break down mold and algae rather than just covering them temporarily.

Rinse and Final Inspection

After the solution has done its work, we rinse thoroughly from top to bottom using a gentle fan spray. We do a final walk of the property with the homeowner (or send photo documentation if you're away) so you can see exactly what was cleaned. Landscaping and hardscaping adjacent to the house get a final rinse as well.

Siding Types We Clean

Vinyl Siding: The most common siding type in Lancaster County. Requires soft wash only — high pressure damages seams and panel lock joints. We adjust concentration based on how oxidized or stained the surface is.

Brick: Lancaster County has a lot of older brick homes, particularly in Lancaster city and borough neighborhoods. Brick can handle moderate pressure on the masonry itself, but mortar joints on older homes require careful technique to avoid erosion.

Stucco: Traditional stucco and modern EIFS (synthetic stucco) both need soft wash. EIFS especially is vulnerable to water intrusion under high pressure. We treat each differently based on age and condition.

HardiePlank and Fiber Cement: Durable but textured — collects algae in the surface grooves. Soft wash handles this well. We use a slightly higher dwell time on fiber cement due to the texture.

Maintenance Frequency

How Often Lancaster Homes Need Washing

Lancaster County's climate creates specific challenges for home exteriors. Summers are humid — often 70–80% relative humidity for weeks at a stretch — which accelerates algae and mold growth on any north or west-facing surface. Winters are cold and wet, with freeze-thaw cycles that can drive moisture into cracks in cladding and brick.

Most homes in Lancaster, Lititz, Manheim, and Ephrata benefit from professional house washing every 1–3 years. The variables are tree coverage, sun exposure, and siding color. Light-colored vinyl shows biological growth sooner visually; dark siding may have just as much algae but it's harder to see until it streaks.

A good rule of thumb: if you can see green or dark discoloration on any face of the house, it's time. If you're preparing to sell, paint, or re-caulk, a thorough wash should always be the first step.

Signs Your Home Needs Washing

Don't wait for the whole exterior to look dirty. These early signs mean it's time to call:

  • Green tint on shaded siding panels
  • Dark streaks running down from gutters
  • Black or gray mottling on north-facing walls
  • Musty smell near foundation plantings
  • Oxidation (chalky film) on older paint
  • Cobwebs and insect buildup around soffits
  • Preparing to repaint or re-side
  • More than 2 years since last cleaning
