How Often Should You Power Wash in Lancaster PA?

Central PA humidity, Lancaster's mold season, tree coverage, and a housing stock full of brick and stucco all affect the answer. Here's a surface-by-surface schedule from a local contractor.

The national advice you'll find online — "power wash your house once or twice a year" — is technically fine, but it ignores what actually matters: where you live. Lancaster County's specific climate, tree cover, and housing stock all change the right answer for your home. This guide is built for Lancaster homeowners, not a generic audience.

Why Lancaster PA Is Different

Lancaster sits in a humid continental climate zone. Summers are hot and humid — average July dewpoints run in the mid-60s — and the county's rural tree cover keeps moisture on siding, decks, and roofs longer than suburban markets in drier areas. That combination creates ideal conditions for:

  • Black algae (Gloeocapsa magma) — the dark streaking you see on north-facing roof sections across Lancaster County
  • Green algae and moss on north-facing siding, particularly on older homes with less direct sun exposure
  • Mold and mildew in shaded areas: under eaves, on decks surrounded by trees, and on fences

Lancaster also has a mold season that peaks in late summer and early fall — August through October — when warm temperatures combine with high humidity and heavy leaf cover. Homes in rural corridors like Conestoga, Willow Street, and Strasburg with mature oak and maple canopy see noticeably faster buildup than homes in open suburban subdivisions in East Hempfield or Manheim Township.

The bottom line for most Lancaster homes: once per year is the minimum, and shaded or tree-covered properties often need washing every 9 months to stay ahead of buildup.

Lancaster's Older Housing Stock: What It Means for Washing Frequency

Lancaster County has one of the highest concentrations of pre-1950 housing in Pennsylvania. The borough of Lancaster, Lititz, Ephrata, Manheim, and Columbia all have significant inventories of 18th and 19th century brick row homes, fieldstone farmhouses, and early 20th century bungalows. These homes were built before modern siding materials existed, and their exterior surfaces behave very differently under pressure than vinyl-sided homes from the 1990s.

Brick and Mortar: The Frequency Factor

Older brick homes in Lancaster City and the surrounding boroughs often have mortar that's 50 to 100+ years old. This mortar is more porous and more vulnerable to pressure damage than modern mortar mixes. The good news: it doesn't need to be washed more often than modern siding — but it needs the right method. High-pressure washing erodes old mortar joints. Soft washing every 2–3 years maintains a clean facade without touching the mortar.

Stucco and Parged Stone

A significant share of mid-20th century Lancaster homes — particularly those built between 1950 and 1980 in suburbs around Lancaster City — have stucco over block or parged stone exteriors. These surfaces develop hairline cracks as they age, and high-pressure water drives directly into those cracks. We recommend soft washing every 2 years for stucco homes, combined with a visual inspection for cracks before and after washing.

Best Time of Year to Power Wash in Lancaster PA

Timing matters. Here's what actually works in Central PA:

  • Late spring (May–early June): Best overall window. Pollen season has ended, temperatures are comfortable for the cleaning solution to activate, and you're ahead of the peak mold season. This is when we do the most house washing in Lancaster County.
  • Early fall (September–October): Second-best window. Before winter, warm enough for solution dwell time, and you remove the summer's algae growth before it sets in over winter.
  • Avoid July–August: Peak heat accelerates solution dry time, which reduces effectiveness. Also the busiest booking window — schedule will fill early.
  • Avoid December–March: Water in cracks and gaps can freeze and expand, causing damage. Cleaning solution doesn't activate well below 50°F.

Signs You've Waited Too Long

You don't need to watch a calendar — your home will tell you when it needs washing. Watch for these on Lancaster-area properties:

  • Black or dark grey streaking on roof shingles (Gloeocapsa magma — extremely common in Lancaster County)
  • Green tinge on north-facing siding or under eaves (algae accumulation in shaded areas)
  • Concrete driveway has darkened significantly or shows biological green/black growth
  • Deck boards are greying or showing mold spots
  • Mortar on brick home is visibly dirty, but don't wait until there's heavy biological growth — that penetrates deeper

If you can see the green: you waited too long. Annual washing prevents buildup from becoming staining — staining that requires more aggressive (and sometimes more damaging) treatment to remove.

FAQ

How often should you power wash a house in Lancaster PA?

Most Lancaster PA homes benefit from power washing once a year. With Lancaster's humidity, tree coverage, and mold season, annual washing prevents buildup from becoming staining. Homes with significant shade, north-facing siding, or surrounded by mature trees may need washing every 9 months.

What time of year is best to power wash in Lancaster PA?

Late spring (May–June) is the ideal window for most Lancaster homes — after pollen season but before summer heat. Fall (September–October) is the second-best time, before winter freezes set in. Avoid washing in freezing temperatures — water in cracks and gaps can expand and cause damage.

How often should I clean my roof in Lancaster PA?

Every 2–3 years for most asphalt roofs in Lancaster County. Central PA's humidity and black algae (Gloeocapsa magma) means Lancaster homes see more roof staining than drier climates. Soft washing (not pressure washing) is the correct method — it kills the algae rather than just removing what's visible.

Does Lancaster's older housing stock need more frequent washing?

Not more frequent — but the method matters more. Older brick, stucco, and parged stone homes require soft washing rather than high-pressure washing. Annual soft washing maintains these surfaces without damaging mortar or stucco.
