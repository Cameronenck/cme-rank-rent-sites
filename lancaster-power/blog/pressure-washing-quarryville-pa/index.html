Pressure Washing in Quarryville, PA — Southern Lancaster County Service, Honest Prices

June 2026 • Lancaster Power Washing Pros • 27 min read

Quarryville sits about fifteen miles south of Lancaster city, right in the heart of southern Lancaster County. It's a smaller borough than most of the towns I work in, but it generates steady business — a mix of older downtown homes, newer residential streets on the outskirts of the borough, and a lot of rural properties in the surrounding townships that need attention you won't find a larger company willing to travel for. I cover Quarryville and the surrounding area regularly: Drumore, Providence, Bart, Eden, and the communities down toward the Maryland line.

The surface conditions down here are different from Lancaster city or the northern townships. Southern Lancaster County sits in the Octoraro Creek watershed, and the humidity levels that come with that drainage corridor mean biological growth establishes faster and harder than in drier parts of the county. Combine that with the significant pollen and agricultural dust load from the surrounding farmland and you've got siding, driveways, and roofs that collect staining at an above-average rate. If your home in Quarryville hasn't had a professional wash in the last two years, there's a good chance it shows — even if you've gotten used to looking at it.

House Washing in Quarryville

The most common job I do in Quarryville is house washing on properties with vinyl siding that's gone gray-green on the north face and along the foundation line. Green algae, mildew streaks, and the general gray film that develops from pollen and atmospheric dust are all there — and down in southern Lancaster County, they tend to be worse than what I see in the northern part of the county because the moisture lingers longer between dry spells.

Soft washing is the right approach for vinyl. I don't use high pressure on siding — it drives water behind panels, cracks caulk around windows and doors, and doesn't actually treat what's causing the problem. Soft washing uses low pressure and a biodegradable cleaning solution that kills the biological growth at the root. The surface stays clean noticeably longer because the organism is dead rather than just temporarily removed.

For the older brick homes in the downtown — and there are some good-looking ones along State Street and the side streets off it — I'm careful about pressure levels and chemistry. Old brick construction, particularly pre-1950 work, often has mortar that's softer than the brick face itself. I'll check it before I set up equipment. The last thing anyone wants is to clean the grime off an old building and take mortar with it. We soft wash these surfaces with appropriate solution concentration and rinse carefully.

Driveway and Concrete Cleaning in Quarryville

Driveways around Quarryville pick up a different kind of load than urban properties. Pollen from crop fields during late spring, harvest dust in late summer and fall, and the general grime that blows off agricultural land over the course of a year embeds into concrete surfaces in ways that a homeowner trying to pressure wash it themselves rarely fully addresses. We pre-treat with a commercial degreaser where oil contamination is present — typical on driveway aprons and garage pads — let the chemistry do its work, then wash.

We use a commercial rotary surface cleaner on flat concrete. It produces even, consistent coverage across the full width and eliminates the striping pattern you get from sweeping a single wand across the surface. On older Quarryville driveways with fifty or sixty years of Pennsylvania freeze-thaw cycling in them, the surface has opened up enough that soil and biological growth work deep into the pores. The results after cleaning are always significant, even on heavily weathered concrete that hasn't been touched in years.

Deck and Fence Washing in Quarryville

Decks on southern Lancaster County properties deal with significant pollen loads in spring and a steady accumulation of organic debris through the growing season. If the deck sits under any tree canopy at all, the board gaps are filling up with decomposing leaf and pollen material that holds moisture and accelerates wood deterioration. Even composite decking, which handles moisture better than wood, will go visibly green and stained when organic debris sits on it through multiple wet-dry cycles.

If you're planning to restain or reseal the deck this season — a common project for Quarryville homeowners in late spring or early summer — cleaning comes first. Stain applied over a dirty, algae-contaminated surface bonds poorly and peels early. I've seen restain jobs that looked rough by late summer simply because the prep wasn't done right. We can clean the deck and get it to the condition it needs to be in for a stain application, or handle the cleaning as a standalone service if you're maintaining a previously sealed surface.

Roof Soft Washing in Quarryville

The dark streaking on asphalt shingle roofs throughout Quarryville and the surrounding southern Lancaster County communities is Gloeocapsa magma — a cyanobacterium that feeds on the limestone filler manufacturers use in shingle composition. It grows in the humid conditions that the Octoraro watershed provides, and once it establishes on a roof, it spreads year over year without treatment. From the ground it can look like weathering or dirt, but it's a living organism that's actively consuming shingle material.

Soft washing is the only correct approach for roof cleaning. A low-pressure application of a specialized chemical solution kills the organism and the residue rinses off over the following weeks of rain. High-pressure roof washing — which some companies still offer — blows shingle granules off the surface, damages the waterproofing layer, and voids most manufacturer warranties. I don't pressure wash roofs. Low pressure, proper chemistry, and patience are what produces a clean roof that stays clean.
