Pressure Washing for Rental Properties in Lancaster PA: A Landlord's Guide

Turnover cleaning, tenant move-out exterior prep, cost per unit, scheduling around occupied units, and what landlords should skip

A rental property's exterior is the first thing a prospective tenant judges, and the last thing a landlord has time to think about. Exterior cleaning lands at the bottom of the list, right up until a walkthrough shows a stained driveway and grimy siding on a unit that should have rented last week. This guide covers how landlords and property managers in Lancaster, York, and Lebanon County use pressure washing to shorten vacancy and protect property value.

Why Rental Exteriors Deteriorate Faster Than Owner-Occupied Homes

A rental exterior takes the same weather as any other home in Lancaster County, but it gets less attention. Tenants mow around the edges and rarely hose down a walkway. Mulch and leaf litter pile against the siding in the fall and stay there. That neglect is why rentals reach a tired look years before an owner-occupied house of the same age. South-central Pennsylvania makes it worse: humid summers and mature street trees feed mold, mildew, and algae on siding and concrete, while winter road salt stains the first few feet of the driveway and foundation. Left through a few cycles, growth bonds tighter and stains set deeper, so the eventual cleaning costs more than a routine annual wash would have.

The Real Cost of a Dirty Rental: Vacancy, Not Cleaning

Landlords tend to frame exterior cleaning as a cost to minimize. The more useful way to see it is as a lever on vacancy. A unit that shows well rents faster, and a week of lost rent dwarfs the price of a wash. A clean driveway, bright siding, and a fresh walkway signal that the property is managed, which raises the quality of applicants.

There is a quieter cost too: deferred maintenance. Grime on siding traps moisture, and moisture drives paint failure, rot, and stained trim. Cleaning on a schedule protects the finishes you have already paid for, which matters most for landlords holding properties long term.

Turnover Cleaning: What to Wash Between Tenants

The make-ready window is short, so turnover cleaning should be targeted at the surfaces a new tenant sees on the walkthrough and in the listing photos:

  • Front walkway and stoop — the first three feet of the property, and the one people see before they reach the door.
  • Driveway and parking pads — oil drips, rust stains, and tire marks read as long-term neglect even when the unit is spotless inside.
  • Street-facing siding — the elevation that shows in the listing photo. Full house washing is worth it when the unit rents by photo, which most do.
  • Decks, patios, and porches — outdoor space photographs well and shows poorly when stained or green.

Skip deep specialty work at turnover unless it is genuinely blocking the rental. Roof cleaning and rust treatment are lower priority, and structural problems are repairs, not cleanings. Our driveway cleaning service and house washing service are the two most common calls at turnover.

Practical tip: Bundle the exterior wash into the same week as interior turnover rather than after it. Cleaning before the photos are taken means the listing shows the property at its best from day one, and it avoids a second trip back to the property once the unit is already listed.

Cleaning Around Occupied Units

Most rental exterior cleaning happens with tenants still living in the unit. Exterior washing needs no entry, so the only coordination is parking and access to outdoor water. We ask that vehicles be moved from driveways and stalls for the visit and that windows and doors stay closed, with a heads-up through the landlord a day or two ahead.

For multi-unit buildings in Lancaster, Columbia, and Mount Joy, wash the whole property in one visit rather than unit by unit. One setup, one water source, one route around the building, and the common areas and entryways get done alongside the individual units. Scheduling: The Annual Route That Solves the Problem

Exterior cleaning slides off the list because it has no fixed date. Nothing forces the wash until a walkthrough goes badly or a tenant complains. The fix is to put it on a schedule. Landlords with one or two units usually pick a recurring annual date in the same month each year, often spring, so the property looks its best heading into prime leasing season. Landlords with a portfolio group properties by borough and wash them across a set week, which keeps travel tight and the per-unit price down. Turnover cleanings slot into the make-ready window as they come up.

Which Surfaces Matter Most on a Rental

Concrete: driveways, walkways, and parking

Concrete is where rentals age fastest in a tenant's eyes. Oil drips, rust from road salt, and organic staining on shaded walkways all read as neglect. Concrete also takes pressure washing well, making it the highest-impact, lowest-risk surface to clean. Our driveway cleaning service covers most Lancaster County rentals.

Siding: vinyl, aluminum, and painted wood

Siding should almost always be soft washed, not pressure washed. The street-facing elevation shows in a listing photo, and a low-pressure wash with a cleaning solution removes green and black growth without driving water behind the siding or stripping paint. Pressure washing vinyl at close range causes streaking and damage that becomes a repair bill.

Decks, gutters, and foundation lines

Wood decks need a low-pressure soft wash and, ideally, a sealer to hold the result; see our deck cleaning cost guide. The dirt band along the foundation and the streaks down a gutter face are the other two visible signs a rental has not been maintained. Gutter brightening removes the tiger stripes on the outside of the run, and a foundation-line wash clears splash staining at the base of the siding. Is Rental Exterior Cleaning a Deductible Expense?

For most landlords, exterior cleaning counts as routine maintenance rather than a capital improvement, because it restores the property to its prior condition instead of adding value or extending its useful life. Routine maintenance is generally deductible in the year incurred on a Schedule E, while improvements are depreciated over years. Cleaning a driveway is maintenance; repaving it is likely an improvement. Confirm with your accountant before filing.
