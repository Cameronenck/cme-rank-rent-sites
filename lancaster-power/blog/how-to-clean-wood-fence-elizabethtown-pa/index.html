How to Clean a Wood Fence in Elizabethtown PA

Soft wash versus pressure washing, why wood fences turn green and gray, how to seal for a longer-lasting result, and what it costs in Lancaster County

A wood fence takes more abuse than any other surface on your property. It faces the sun, sits in the wet shade of your lawn, and never gets rinsed by anything except rain. That is why so many fences in Elizabethtown and across Lancaster County end up green-streaked on one side and silver-gray on the other. This guide covers how to clean a wood fence the right way, when to soft wash instead of pressure wash, and how sealing makes the job last.

Why Wood Fences Get Dirty So Fast in Lancaster County

Elizabethtown and the surrounding townships sit in a humid pocket of central Pennsylvania. Summer humidity, mature shade trees, and heavy morning dew give mold, mildew, and algae exactly what they need, and a wood fence holds moisture in its grain far longer than concrete. Two different problems show up on wood:

  • Green or black growth is mildew and algae. It feeds on moisture and shade, typically on the tree-shaded side of a fence, and it lifts with a proper soft wash.
  • Silvering or graying is UV damage. Sunlight breaks down lignin in the wood fibers and the surface weathers to pale gray. Cleaning and brightening restores much of the original tone; sealing keeps it from coming back.

Soft Wash vs Pressure Wash for Wood Fences

Soft washing: the correct method for wood

A soft wash applies a cleaning solution at low pressure, lets it dwell and kill the mildew and algae at the root, then rinses it away. Low pressure is the whole point: wood fibers are soft relative to concrete or brick, and the cleaning happens chemically rather than mechanically. Our house washing service uses this same low-pressure approach.

Pressure washing: where it goes wrong

High pressure aimed at wood causes damage that is easy to spot once you know it. The grain raises and furs, boards splinter at the edges, and water is forced into the joints where it sits and starts rot from the inside. It can also strip a full season of finish in one pass. Be cautious of any quote built around it.

The one-second test: If a contractor plans to hit your wood fence with a high-pressure wand at close range, get a second opinion. Cleaning wood is a chemistry job, not a pressure job.

What about vinyl, composite, and metal fences

Not every fence is wood. Vinyl and composite take a soft wash too, because high pressure can drive water behind the panel joints and loosen the sections. Aluminum and wrought-iron tolerate controlled pressure but still benefit from a soft wash. Match the method to the surface.

Step-by-Step: How to Clean a Wood Fence

Here is the sequence we use on fences in Elizabethtown, Mount Joy, Manheim, and the rest of Lancaster County.

  1. Clear and protect landscaping. Trim weeds back, cover or wet down plants, and keep pets clear. Cleaning solution that dries on foliage can burn it.
  2. Pre-wet the wood. A quick rinse helps the solution spread evenly and keeps it from drying too fast in direct sun.
  3. Apply the solution. Work bottom-up in sections so drips do not streak over cleaner you already applied. Let it dwell, but do not let it dry.
  4. Agitate stubborn areas. A soft-bristle brush on the lower rails, post caps, and heavy growth loosens what chemistry alone leaves behind.
  5. Rinse top-down. A gentle rinse carries the dead growth away and keeps runoff from re-soiling the sections below.
  6. Treat for graying. If the fence has silvered, apply a wood brightener to even out the tone.
  7. Dry completely, then seal. Wood must be fully dry before any sealer goes on. Never seal a damp fence.

Should You Seal or Stain After Cleaning?

Cleaning without sealing is a temporary fix. An unsealed fence starts growing mildew and weathering again within a season or two, which is why so many fences look gray a year after a cleaning. A clear penetrating sealer locks out moisture and blocks UV graying while keeping the natural wood tone; a tinted stain-sealer also evens out color. Both go on after the fence is fully dry, on a day without rain in the forecast. Our deck and patio washing service applies the same clean-and-protect thinking to wood decks.

How Often Should You Clean a Wood Fence?

Most wood fences in south-central Pennsylvania need cleaning every one to two years. A fence in full shade or backed by mature trees lands on the frequent end, because shade and moisture hold growth longer; one in full sun may stretch to two years, though UV graying still advances. Sealing after cleaning buys a longer interval. Watch for early signs: a green tint on the shaded side or a dull gray cast creeping up the pickets.

Frequently Asked Questions: Cleaning a Wood Fence

Should I pressure wash a wood fence or soft wash it?

Soft wash it. High pressure etches the grain, splinters boards, and drives water into the joints where it can rot the wood from the inside. A low-pressure soft wash kills the mildew and algae first, then a gentle rinse carries it away.

Why is my wood fence turning green or gray?

Green growth is mildew and algae feeding on moisture and shade. Graying is UV damage: sunlight breaks down lignin and the surface weathers to silver-gray. Green lifts with a soft wash; graying needs cleaning plus a brightener.

How much does it cost to have a wood fence cleaned in Elizabethtown PA?

Fence cleaning is priced by linear foot and height, so a six-foot privacy fence costs more per foot than a short picket or split-rail fence. Costs rise with buildup and if you add sealing; bundling with a house wash or deck cleaning lowers the overall price.

Bottom Line for Elizabethtown and Lancaster County Homeowners

A wood fence is one of the easiest exterior surfaces to protect and one of the easiest to damage with the wrong method. Soft wash rather than pressure wash, treat graying with a brightener, seal after it dries, and clean every one to two years before growth takes hold.
