How to Clean Vinyl Siding in Lancaster PA

A homeowner's guide to soft washing, pressure washing, and keeping your siding free of algae and mildew year-round

Green and black streaks on your siding aren't just ugly — they're algae and mildew feeding on Lancaster County's humidity. The good news: vinyl siding is one of the easiest surfaces to clean when you use the right method. This guide covers how to do it safely yourself, when soft washing beats pressure washing, and what it costs to have a local pro handle it.

Why Vinyl Siding Gets Dirty So Fast in Lancaster PA

Lancaster County sits in a humid climate zone with frequent summer rain, heavy morning dew, and long stretches of shade from mature trees. That combination is a perfect recipe for organic growth. Here's what actually builds up on your siding:

  • Green algae — thrives on the north and east sides of homes that stay damp after rain
  • Mildew and mold — black or gray streaks that spread in shaded, low-airflow spots
  • Pollen and dirt — spring blooms and farm dust settle into the textured vinyl surface
  • Insect droppings and cobwebs — common near porch lights and under eaves

Left alone, this growth doesn't just look bad. Over time it can etch into the siding's finish and shorten its life. Regular cleaning protects your home's exterior and its curb appeal.

Signs Your Siding Needs Cleaning Now

Not sure if it's time? Look for these telltale signs on your Lancaster PA home:

  • Green or black discoloration on shaded walls
  • Siding that looks dull or gray compared to a freshly cleaned neighbor's home
  • Dark streaks running down from windows and roof edges
  • Visible cobwebs, dirt, or a chalky film when you run a hand across the surface

Most homes in Lancaster, Lititz, Manheim, and Ephrata should plan for a professional cleaning once a year. Homes with heavy tree cover or north-facing walls may need attention every six to nine months.

Pressure Washing vs. Soft Washing Vinyl Siding

This is the most important decision you'll make, and it's where DIY homeowners most often go wrong.

Pressure Washing

Pressure washing uses high-pressure water to blast dirt away. It works, but on vinyl it carries real risk. Pressure above roughly 1,300 PSI — or water sprayed upward at an angle — can force water behind the siding, into the seams, and into your wall cavity. That trapped moisture leads to mold, rot, and peeling paint inside the home.

Soft Washing

Soft washing uses low pressure (about 100 to 500 PSI) combined with a biodegradable cleaning solution. The solution is applied, allowed to dwell, and then rinsed away. It kills algae and mildew at the root rather than just knocking it off the surface, so results last longer. For vinyl siding, this is the method we recommend for every home from Harrisburg to York to Lebanon.

Rule of thumb: If you're cleaning vinyl siding, soft wash it. Save high pressure for concrete driveways, walkways, and brick patios — surfaces that can take the force.

How to Clean Vinyl Siding Yourself (Step by Step)

If you'd rather tackle it yourself, here's a safe method for Lancaster PA homeowners:

  1. Clear the area. Move patio furniture, cover plants and shrubs, and close all windows and doors.
  2. Protect outlets and fixtures. Cover exterior outlets and light fixtures with plastic and tape.
  3. Apply a siding-safe cleaner. Use a cleaner rated for vinyl, or a soft wash detergent. Work from the bottom up to avoid streaking.
  4. Let it dwell. Allow the solution to sit for 5 to 10 minutes so it can break down algae and mildew.
  5. Rinse with low pressure. Use a garden hose or a washer set under 1,300 PSI, spraying downward from top to bottom.
  6. Inspect. Touch up any remaining spots rather than cranking up the pressure.

DIY is doable for single-story homes in good condition. For two-story homes, steep rooflines, or heavy algae growth, the equipment and ladder work are usually not worth the risk — and a pro's soft wash system does a deeper, longer-lasting job.

Common DIY Mistakes That Damage Siding

We see the same avoidable mistakes across Columbia, Mount Joy, and Elizabethtown:

  • Spraying upward. Water gets forced behind the siding and into the seams.
  • Using too much pressure. High PSI can crack, gouge, and permanently etch vinyl.
  • Using bleach straight. Undiluted bleach can streak and dull the finish, and it harms landscaping.
  • Skipping the rinse. Dried cleaner residue attracts dirt faster than the original grime did.
  • Cleaning in direct sun. Cleaners dry too fast and streak on hot, sunny days.

Best Time of Year to Clean Siding in Lancaster PA

Spring and early fall are the sweet spots. In spring, you wash away winter grime and the pollen that follows. In early fall, you remove summer algae and prep the exterior for winter moisture. Avoid cleaning in freezing temperatures, and skip the hottest midday hours in July and August.

For most Lancaster County homes, a spring cleaning plus a lighter fall touch-up keeps siding looking fresh all year without over-washing the surface.

FAQ: Cleaning Vinyl Siding

Will cleaning my siding damage my plants?

Not if it's done right. A professional soft wash team pre-wets landscaping and uses biodegradable, siding-safe solutions. DIY homeowners should cover plants and rinse them thoroughly after cleaning.

How long does a professional siding cleaning take?

Most single-family homes take one to three hours from setup to rinse, depending on size and condition. Soft washing is efficient, so there's minimal disruption to your day.

Can soft washing remove black streaks permanently?

Soft washing kills the mildew and algae that cause streaks, so they stay away longer than a pressure-only rinse would. No cleaning is permanent — annual maintenance keeps them from returning.

Is vinegar safe for cleaning vinyl siding?

Diluted vinegar can help with light mineral stains, but it won't kill algae or mildew the way a proper soft wash solution does. For organic growth, stick to siding-safe cleaners or call a pro.
