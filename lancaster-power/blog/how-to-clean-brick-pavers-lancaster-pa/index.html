How to Clean Brick Pavers in Lancaster PA

Brick pavers add elegance to your outdoor spaces, but they're magnets for algae, mold, and stains in Lancaster's humid climate. Here's exactly how to clean them safely and keep them looking new.

Why Brick Pavers Are More Challenging to Clean Than Concrete

At first glance, brick pavers might look just like any other hard surface, but they demand special attention. Unlike solid concrete, pavers have deep joint lines filled with sand, and the brick itself is more porous and delicate. That means aggressive pressure washing can:

  • Blast sand from the joints, destabilizing the pavers
  • Pit or chip the brick surface, creating texture damage
  • Force water behind the pavers into the base material
  • Strip the protective "patina" that helps resist future staining

In Lancaster County's humidity, brick pavers also attract algae, mold, and efflorescence (white salt deposits) faster than in drier climates. A patio under tree shade or near downspouts can get dirty in 18 months, especially if there's poor drainage underneath.

The Right Tools for Brick Paver Cleaning

Professional paver cleaning isn't just about pressure — it's about the right technique and equipment. Here's what works:

Surface Cleaners (2,500–3,500 PSI)

This is the gold standard for paver cleaning. A surface cleaner is a rotating head that distributes pressure evenly across a wide area (usually 24–30 inches), preventing the concentrated jet from digging into brick or sand. It leaves a uniform clean and moves across pavers at a consistent pace. We use this for 90% of paver jobs in Lancaster.

Wide Fan Tips (25–40 degrees)

If a surface cleaner isn't available, a wide fan nozzle (40 degrees) at 2,500–3,000 PSI, held at least 12 inches from the surface, can work. Never use a 15-degree or narrower nozzle on pavers — you'll damage them.

Pre-Treatment for Heavy Staining

For pavers with embedded algae, mold, or efflorescence, pre-treating with a biodegradable alkaline cleaner or algaecide 10–15 minutes before washing makes the job easier and faster. This kills organic growth at the root, so it doesn't come back as quickly.

Step-by-Step: How to Clean Brick Pavers Safely

Step 1: Inspect and Test

Before starting the full job, wash a small, inconspicuous area (like the corner of a patio) at the pressure you plan to use. This tells you whether the pavers and joints can handle it. Older or softer brick may need lower pressure.

Step 2: Clear the Area

Remove loose debris, leaves, and dirt with a broom or leaf blower. This prevents debris from becoming embedded during washing and reduces cleanup afterward.

Step 3: Pre-Treat Heavy Stains

If pavers are heavily stained or covered in algae, spray a pre-treatment cleaner and let it sit for 10–15 minutes. Don't let it dry completely — keep it moist. This softens the stain and makes pressure washing more effective.

Step 4: Pressure Wash with a Surface Cleaner

Set your pressure washer to 2,500–3,500 PSI (depending on paver age and condition) and use a surface cleaner. Move steadily in overlapping passes, like you're mowing a lawn. This ensures even coverage and prevents missed spots.

Step 5: Rinse Thoroughly

After washing, do a final rinse at lower pressure (1,500–2,000 PSI) to remove soap and loosened dirt. Use a wide fan nozzle for this step.

Step 6: Let Dry and Inspect

Allow 24–48 hours for full drying before assessing the results. Wet pavers look different than dry ones, so don't judge the clean until they've dried completely.

Should You Seal Brick Pavers After Cleaning?

Sealing is optional but highly recommended in Lancaster. A quality penetrating sealer:

  • Blocks water and salt intrusion, preventing efflorescence
  • Reduces staining from oil, dirt, and organic growth
  • Extends the time between cleanings by 1–2 years
  • Enhances the color slightly and gives a subtle protective sheen

The downside: sealers need to be reapplied every 3–5 years. The cost is typically $100–$300 depending on the paved area's size. In Lancaster's humid climate, it's usually worth it.

How Often Should You Clean Brick Pavers in Lancaster?

For most Lancaster properties, cleaning every 2–3 years keeps pavers looking great. However:

  • High-traffic driveways: Every 1–2 years (oil stains and dirt accumulate faster)
  • Shaded patios: Every 1–2 years (algae and mold thrive in shade)
  • Full-sun spaces: Every 3 years (UV light slows organic growth)
  • Near trees: Every 1–2 years (leaves trap moisture and promote algae)

Regular cleaning is actually cheaper than letting stains build up heavily, which then requires more aggressive (and expensive) treatment.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using a pressure washer without a surface cleaner: You'll gouge the brick and blow out the sand holding pavers in place.
  • Pressure washing immediately after rain: Pavers are waterlogged and more susceptible to damage. Wait 24 hours.
  • Ignoring the joints: Weeds and moss grow in joints first. Treat them before full washing.
  • Skipping sealing: In Lancaster's humidity, unsealed pavers stain and need cleaning more frequently.
  • Over-pressuring old pavers: Vintage or handmade brick can't handle 3,500 PSI. Always start lower and test first.

Frequently Asked Questions About Brick Paver Cleaning

Can you use a pressure washer on brick pavers in Lancaster PA?
Yes, but with care. Brick pavers can handle pressure washing at 2,500–3,500 PSI, but you need to use a surface cleaner or wide fan tip to avoid pitting the surface or blasting sand from the joints. Many contractors make the mistake of pointing a narrower nozzle directly at the pavers, which can damage the brick and loosen the sand that keeps them stable. For older or more delicate pavers, start lower (2,000 PSI) and test an inconspicuous area first.
How much does it cost to pressure wash brick pavers in Lancaster County?
Paver cleaning in Lancaster County typically ranges from $300 to $800 depending on the size of the patio, driveway, or walkway, and the level of soiling. A small patio (100–150 sq ft) runs $200–$400. A large driveway or patio (300–500 sq ft) runs $500–$800. Heavily stained or weedy pavers cost more due to pre-treatment and extra time. We offer free on-site estimates for all paver cleaning jobs.
Do brick pavers need sealing after cleaning?
Not required, but highly recommended. After cleaning, a quality paver sealer (water- or penetrating-type) protects the brick from stains, UV fading, and moisture damage. It also makes weeds and algae less likely to return. In Lancaster's humid summers, sealing adds 1–2 years of protection. A sealant costs $100–$300 depending on area size and seal quality, but it extends the life of your pavers significantly.
What are those white crystals (efflorescence) on my brick pavers?
That's efflorescence — salt deposits that naturally emerge as moisture in the brick evaporates. It's harmless but looks unsightly. Pressure washing removes it temporarily, but it can return, especially in Lancaster's humidity. A sealer after cleaning prevents it from coming back by blocking water intrusion. Some contractors apply an acidic wash before sealing, which removes efflorescence more completely, but this requires professional care to avoid damaging the brick.
How often should you clean brick pavers in Lancaster PA?
For patios and walkways, every 2–3 years keeps them looking fresh. Driveways exposed to vehicle stains may need cleaning every 1–2 years. If pavers are under tree cover or in shaded, humid areas (common around Lancaster), algae and mold return faster — you might need cleaning every 1–2 years. Regular maintenance is cheaper than letting heavy staining build up, which requires more aggressive (and expensive) treatment.
