Pressure Washing Before Selling Your Home in Lancaster PA

Which exterior cleaning projects actually move the needle on curb appeal, which ones waste money, and the order to do them in before your listing photos

Buyers decide more in the first thirty seconds than in the next thirty minutes. In a Lancaster County market where a buyer may tour three similar homes in one weekend, the one that looks maintained from the curb has a real edge. This guide covers which exterior surfaces to clean before listing, which to leave alone, the method for each material, roughly what it costs, and when to schedule it.

Why Curb Appeal Still Decides Listings in Lancaster County

A buyer walking up to a home is doing an unconscious maintenance audit. Every dark streak on the siding and every green haze on the sidewalk is a small question they have to resolve, and those questions add up into a vague impression that the home has been let go. Clean exterior surfaces do the opposite: they remove objections before they form, so the buyer can focus on the layout, the light, and the features you actually want to sell.

This is not about deception. Mildew on siding and algae on a north-facing driveway say nothing about a home's roof, mechanicals, or structure. Cleaning off summer surface growth simply lets the home show as it actually is.

The Order of Operations: Do It in This Sequence

Cleaning in the wrong order wastes the work. Wash the driveway last and runoff from the siding above re-soils the concrete you just cleaned. Work top-down and outside-in:

  1. Roof first. Anything that rinses off the roof lands on the siding and driveway below.
  2. House washing second. Siding, trim, gutters, soffits, and fascia, before the hard surfaces.
  3. Gutters. Clear debris and brighten the exterior, one of the most visible maintenance signals.
  4. Deck and patio. Clean the outdoor living spaces, a major selling point here.
  5. Hard surfaces last. Driveway, walkway, front steps, and any pavers that collect runoff from above.
Sequence matters: A driveway cleaning done before the house wash can look like half a job by the time the runoff settles.

What to Pressure Wash Before Listing, and How

Not every surface takes the same method. Using high pressure where it does not belong is the most common way sellers turn a cosmetic job into a repair.

Concrete driveways, walkways, and front steps

The highest-impact, most cost-effective cleaning before listing. A dirty driveway is the first thing a buyer sees and judges. Concrete takes pressure washing well because the surface is hard and uniform. A professional job uses a surface cleaner and pre-treatment for organic growth so the result is even rather than striped. See our driveway cleaning service.

House siding, trim, and gutters

Vinyl, fiber cement, and painted wood siding all collect the same green and black growth off Lancaster County humidity and tree cover. Siding should be soft washed rather than high pressured: the goal is to kill and rinse the growth, not blast the cladding. High pressure forces water behind panels and strips paint from trim. Our house washing service uses the low-pressure approach that protects the finish.

Decks, patios, and porches

Outdoor living space sells homes here, and a gray, algae-streaked deck reads as a project rather than a feature. Wood decks need low-pressure cleaning and, if the finish has worn, re-sealing. Composite needs a gentler touch still. Paver patios need controlled pressure and attention to joint sand. See our deck and patio cleaning service.

Roofs and other soft-wash surfaces

If your roof has black streaks or green growth, do not let anyone pressure wash it. Asphalt shingles lose protective granules under high pressure, and a buyer's inspector will flag the damage. Roofs should be soft washed with a solution that kills the algae and lets rain rinse it away. The same goes for stucco and aged painted surfaces.

Which Exterior Projects Are Worth It Before Selling

Every dollar spent prepping a home should return more than it costs. Exterior cleaning sits near the top of that list because the cost is modest and the visibility is total.

  • Worth it — driveway and walkway cleaning. Lowest cost per visible square foot.
  • Worth it — house washing. Removes the objection that the home looks tired.
  • Worth it — deck and patio cleaning. Turns a liability into a feature.
  • Worth it — gutter brightening. Tiger-striped gutters are a common "needs work" signal and cheap to fix.
  • Situational — roof soft washing. Worth it if streaking is prominent or the roof is near end of life.
  • Skip — pressure washing painted or aged wood. High risk of stripping paint and adding to a repair list.

What Cleaning Cannot Fix

Exterior cleaning improves presentation; it does not repair damage. Cracks, rot, or soft deck boards become more visible after cleaning, not less. The most valuable part of a professional pre-listing clean is often the assessment, which flags the loose caulk or soft board a buyer's inspector will find anyway.

When to Schedule It Before Your Listing Photos

The sweet spot is one to two weeks before your listing photos. That window leaves surfaces fully dry and gives you time to act on anything the cleaning reveals. Clean the siding in April and shoot photos in June and pollen has already dulled the result. For sellers in Lancaster city, Lititz, Ephrata, Manheim, Mount Joy, and the surrounding townships, book the cleaning the moment you decide to list. Spring and early summer are peak season for both listings and exterior cleaning.

Working With Your Agent on Timing

Ask your agent two things: what day the listing photos are scheduled, and whether competing listings present particular surfaces well. Work backward from those answers to a cleaning date. The homes that show well are the ones where the seller did not stop at cleaning the inside.

Frequently Asked Questions: Pre-Listing Exterior Cleaning

What should I pressure wash before selling my house in Lancaster PA?

Focus on what a buyer sees from the curb and on the walk to the front door: the concrete driveway and walkway, the front steps, the siding, and the deck or patio. Roofs, gutters, and painted or aged wood need different methods and should be left to a professional.

Does pressure washing a house before selling actually increase the sale price?

It does not change the appraisal value, but it changes how buyers perceive the home. A clean exterior signals a well-maintained property and reduces the objections a buyer brings to the table, which helps a listing show better in a competitive Lancaster County market.

Should I pressure wash my deck or roof before listing my home?

No. Wood decks and asphalt shingle roofs should never be hit with high pressure, which furs and splinters wood and strips the protective granules from shingles. Decks need a soft wash and, if worn, re-sealing. Roofs need a soft wash that kills the algae rather than blasting it off.

How much does exterior cleaning cost before selling a home in Lancaster PA?

It depends on how much you combine. A driveway and walkway cleaning typically runs in the low hundreds, house washing is priced by size and stories, and a combined package is almost always cheaper than booking each surface separately.

How far in advance of listing should I have the exterior cleaned?

One to two weeks before your listing photos, so surfaces dry fully and you can address anything the cleaning exposes. Cleaning too far ahead means the driveway and siding collect pollen again before buyers see them.

Can I pressure wash my house siding myself before an open house?

On durable siding like vinyl, a careful homeowner can do a reasonable job. The risk is on painted wood, stucco, and aged caulk, where water gets behind the cladding and paint strips off. If your home is painted wood or has aging trim, have it professionally soft washed.

Bottom Line for Lancaster County Sellers

Exterior cleaning is one of the cheapest, highest-visibility improvements you can make before listing, and it works because it removes objections rather than adding features. Clean the surfaces buyers actually see, use the right method for each material, work top-down, and schedule it one to two weeks before your photos. Skip high pressure on anything painted, aged, or shingled.
